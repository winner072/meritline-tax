Who usually needs Form 5472?

Most often, a U.S. LLC with a single owner who lives outside the United States. Since 2017, these LLCs file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year they have transactions with their owner, and paying the formation fees or funding the bank account is enough. That is why an LLC with no income usually still files. U.S. corporations that are at least 25% foreign-owned also file it, attached to their Form 1120. For the full rules, read Form 5472 explained.

You usually do not need Form 5472 in these cases:

  • You are a U.S. citizen or green card holder and own the LLC alone. You are a U.S. person, so your LLC is not foreign-owned. See who files Form 5472 and who does not.
  • Your LLC has two members, one U.S. and one foreign. By default it is a partnership, so it files Form 1065 instead.
  • Nothing at all moved between you and the LLC during the year, not even state or registered agent fees you paid yourself. This is rare, and the first year almost always needs a return. See Form 5472 with no income.

What the checker asks, and why

  1. Type of companyForm 5472 applies to U.S. LLCs and corporations, so the first question confirms you have a U.S. entity.
  2. Who owns itOne owner outside the U.S. usually means Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120. Two or more owners usually means a partnership return. U.S. owners generally do not file Form 5472.
  3. Money between you and the companyForm 5472 depends on transactions between the company and you, your family, or another company you own, not on income. A fee you paid for the company yourself counts, and so does money you put into its bank account or took out. If nothing at all moved during the year, Form 5472 may not be due.
  4. State of registrationEach state has its own annual report or tax. Wyoming, Delaware, and Florida require yearly filings, while New Mexico does not require an annual report from LLCs.
  5. Filing historyEach missed Form 5472 is a separate late return, so we flag catch-up filing early.
  6. Accounts outside the U.S.Accounts totalling more than $10,000 at any time in the year can trigger an FBAR.
  7. IRS lettersA notice has a response deadline, so it moves to the top of your checklist.

How accurate is the result?

It reflects the general rules for foreign-owned U.S. companies and is a reliable starting point. It is not tax advice. We confirm your exact requirements, including your tax year and filing history, when we prepare your quote.

My LLC had no income. Will the checker still say I need to file?

Usually, yes. Form 5472 depends on transactions between you and the company, such as paying formation fees or funding the bank account, not on whether the LLC made money. If nothing at all moved between you and the company during the year, the checker says Form 5472 may not be due, and we confirm this with you.

What if the checker shows missed years?

Missed years can be filed late, ideally before the IRS contacts you and with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one. See how Form 5472 penalty abatement works.

When and where is Form 5472 filed?

For calendar-year LLCs, Form 5472 and the pro forma Form 1120 are generally due April 15. Filing Form 7004 by April 15 extends the deadline to October 15. A foreign-owned LLC cannot e-file Form 5472. It is faxed to 855-887-7737 or mailed to the dedicated IRS unit in Ogden, Utah.

What transactions must be reported on Form 5472?

Transactions between the LLC and you or other related parties. These include formation costs you paid personally, money you put into or took out of the LLC, company costs you paid yourself, and loans in either direction. Payments the LLC receives from unrelated customers do not go on Form 5472, because customers are not related parties.
