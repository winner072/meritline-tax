Form 1120 vs. Form 1065: Which Tax Return Does Your U.S. Business File?

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated November 10, 20267 min read

Key points

  • Your federal return depends on how your business is classified for tax purposes, not just its name.
  • A multi-member LLC is treated as a partnership by default and files Form 1065.
  • A C corporation, or an LLC that elects to be taxed as one, files Form 1120.
  • A foreign-owned single-member LLC files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120.

Which tax return your business files depends on how it is classified for federal tax purposes, which is not always the same as how it was formed with the state.

The short answer

Business typeFederal return (by default)
Single-member LLC, U.S. ownerNo separate return. Income is reported on the owner's Form 1040, usually on Schedule C.
Single-member LLC, foreign ownerForm 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120.
Multi-member LLCForm 1065, with a Schedule K-1 for each member.
General or limited partnershipForm 1065, with a Schedule K-1 for each partner.
C corporationForm 1120.
LLC that elected corporate tax treatmentForm 1120 (after filing Form 8832).
S corporationForm 1120-S (after filing Form 2553).

Form 1065: the partnership return

Form 1065 is filed by partnerships and by LLCs with two or more members that have not chosen to be taxed as a corporation. A partnership generally does not pay federal income tax itself. Instead, its income, deductions, and credits "pass through" to the partners.

  • Each partner receives a Schedule K-1 showing their share of the results, which they report on their own return.
  • For calendar-year partnerships, Form 1065 is due March 15, with an extension to September 15 available through Form 7004.
  • A U.S. partnership must file Form 1065 for any year in which it has income, or expenses that count as deductions or credits for federal tax purposes, even if the amounts are small. Under the IRS instructions for Form 1065, a year with no income and no such expenses generally does not require a return. Late-filing penalties are charged per partner, per month.

Partnerships with foreign partners may have additional reporting and withholding obligations, so it is important to plan for these early.

Form 1120: the corporate return

Form 1120 is filed by C corporations, including LLCs that have elected to be taxed as corporations. A C corporation is a separate taxpayer:

  • It pays federal corporate income tax on its profits at a flat rate of 21%.
  • Profits paid out to shareholders as dividends may be taxed again at the shareholder level.
  • For calendar-year corporations, Form 1120 is due April 15, with an extension to October 15 available through Form 7004.

Where does the foreign-owned single-member LLC fit?

A single-member LLC owned by a non-U.S. person is normally a "disregarded entity", so it does not file a regular Form 1120 or Form 1065. However, it must file Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120 to report transactions with its owner. It is a common source of confusion: the LLC uses the Form 1120 as a cover page, but it does not become a C corporation or pay corporate tax by doing so.

Can you choose how your business is taxed?

Yes, within limits. An LLC can elect to be taxed as a C corporation by filing Form 8832, or as an S corporation by filing Form 2553. Note that S corporations cannot have nonresident alien shareholders, so the S corporation option is generally not available to foreign owners.

The right choice depends on your profits, where you live, how you plan to take money out of the business, and your state's rules. It is worth getting advice before you elect, because changing classification later can be restricted.

Remember your state return

Your federal return is only part of the picture. Many states require their own partnership, corporate, or franchise tax filings, and deadlines and fees vary from state to state.
