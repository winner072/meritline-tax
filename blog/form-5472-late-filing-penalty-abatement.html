Form 5472 Late Filing Penalty: How Abatement Works for Foreign-Owned LLCs

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorOctober 25, 20267 min read

The penalty for filing Form 5472 late is $25,000 per form, per tax year, and it applies even when the LLC earned nothing. It is one of the largest fixed penalties in the U.S. tax system for a small company. It is not, however, the end of the story. The IRS can remove, or abate, the penalty where the owner shows reasonable cause, and in some cases other relief is available. What you do first, and in what order, has a large effect on the outcome.

The essentials

  • Base penalty: $25,000 for each Form 5472 that is late, missing, or substantially incomplete.
  • It can grow: a further $25,000 for each 30-day period the failure continues more than 90 days after the IRS notifies you. There is no maximum.
  • Main route to relief: a written reasonable cause explanation, signed under penalties of perjury.
  • Best position: filing every missing year before the IRS contacts you.

How the penalty is calculated

Section 6038A of the Internal Revenue Code sets the penalty, and the IRS Instructions for Form 5472 apply it to foreign-owned single-member LLCs. The amounts add up quickly:

SituationPotential penalty
One tax year filed late$25,000
Three tax years never filed$75,000, one penalty per year
IRS notice ignored for 150 daysBase penalty plus $50,000 for two further 30-day periods after the first 90 days
Form filed but key items missingCan be treated as not filed, so $25,000

These are maximum exposures, not certain bills. They show why a notice from the IRS should never be left unanswered, and why accuracy matters as much as timing.

Which situation are you in?

The right step depends on where you are today. Most foreign owners we speak to fall into one of these four positions:

Your positionWhat to do now
Never filed, no IRS contactFile every outstanding year together, each with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one. This is the strongest position you can be in.
Filed late, no notice yetKeep proof of the date you filed and the documents behind your explanation. If a notice arrives, you are ready to respond quickly.
Penalty notice receivedNote the response date on the notice and send a written abatement request, with evidence, within that period.
Abatement refusedYou may be able to ask the IRS Independent Office of Appeals to review the decision. Time limits apply, so act promptly.

The three routes to penalty relief

  1. Reasonable causeThe main route. The IRS considers whether you exercised ordinary business care and prudence and were still unable to file on time. Each case is decided on its own facts.
  2. First-time abatement, in narrow casesThis administrative relief does not generally apply to Form 5472. The Internal Revenue Manual extends it only in limited circumstances, broadly where the penalty was assessed systematically with a late Form 1120 that itself qualifies, and the company has a clean record for the three prior years. It is worth asking about, but not relying on.
  3. AppealIf a request is refused, the IRS Independent Office of Appeals can review it. Appeals officers are separate from the team that assessed the penalty.

What the IRS weighs

Usually helpsUsually hurts
Filing promptly once you learned of the requirementWaiting months or years after learning of it
A clean filing history in other yearsRepeated late filings
Reliance on a professional who gave wrong advice, with evidenceA general claim that you did not know, with nothing more
Serious illness, disaster, or loss of records, with datesBeing busy, or not having time
A complete, accurate return filed with the requestA request sent without the missing return

Not knowing about Form 5472 is common among foreign owners, and it is an honest explanation. On its own, the IRS rarely accepts it. It carries more weight alongside other facts, such as being a first-time owner abroad, relying on a formation agent, or acting immediately once you found out.

Anatomy of a strong reasonable cause statement

  1. A clear timelineWhen the LLC was formed, when each return was due, when you learned of the requirement, and when you filed.
  2. The specific reasonWhat actually prevented timely filing, described factually and without exaggeration.
  3. What you did nextThe steps you took once you became aware, and how quickly.
  4. How you will stay compliantFor example, engaging a preparer and setting annual reminders.
  5. Supporting evidenceEmails, advice letters, medical or travel records, attached and referred to.
  6. A signature under penalties of perjuryEvery statement must be true and consistent with your records.

Mistakes that weaken your case

  • Ignoring an IRS notice, which can start the continuation penalty.
  • Using a generic template that does not describe your actual situation.
  • Filing only the latest year and leaving earlier years outstanding.
  • Making claims your documents do not support.
  • Sending a request to the wrong IRS address. Foreign-owned LLCs use a dedicated fax number and Ogden, Utah address.

Frequently asked questions

Is the $25,000 Form 5472 penalty automatic?

It can be assessed when a late return is processed, or later if the IRS finds a missing filing. A reasonable cause statement filed with the late return gives the IRS grounds to consider relief, although penalties can still be assessed and then challenged.

Does first-time abatement apply to Form 5472?

Generally not. The Internal Revenue Manual allows it only in narrow cases linked to a related Form 1120 that qualifies for first-time abatement and a clean three-year history. Reasonable cause is the main route for most foreign owners.

My LLC had no income. Does that reduce the penalty?

No. The penalty is fixed and does not depend on income. A company with no activity beyond formation costs can still face the full $25,000 per year.

Should I wait to see if the IRS notices?

No. Filing before the IRS contacts you is generally the strongest position, and the continuation penalty only starts after IRS notification. Waiting removes options.
