Form 5472 Instructions Explained: The Complete Guide for Foreign Owners of U.S. LLCs

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated November 10, 202610 min read

Form 5472 is the annual IRS information return that a U.S. LLC owned by one foreign person must file, together with a pro forma Form 1120, even when the company earned nothing and owes no U.S. tax. It reports the money and value that moved between the company and its owner during the year. The form itself is short. The rules around it are not, and the penalty for getting it wrong starts at $25,000. This guide explains, in plain language, what the form is, whether it applies to you, what goes on each part, and how to file it correctly.

Form 5472 at a glance

  • Who files: U.S. single-member LLCs wholly owned by a foreign person, and U.S. corporations that are at least 25% foreign-owned.
  • What it reports: transactions between the company and its foreign owner or other related parties, including money paid in, money taken out, and formation costs.
  • When: April 15 for calendar-year companies, or October 15 with a timely Form 7004 extension.
  • How: by fax or mail to a dedicated IRS unit. A foreign-owned LLC cannot e-file it.
  • Penalty: $25,000 per form, per year, for a late, missing, or substantially incomplete return.

Official IRS documents: Form 5472 (PDF) and the Instructions for Form 5472 (Rev. December 2024), also as a PDF. For a line-by-line example, see our completed Form 5472.

What Form 5472 is, and why it exists

Form 5472 is an information return. It does not calculate tax. Its job is to tell the IRS about transactions between a U.S. company and foreign persons connected to it, so the IRS can see money and property crossing the border inside a related group.

For decades, the form applied only to foreign-owned U.S. corporations. That changed with Treasury regulations that took effect for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2017. Since then, a U.S. LLC wholly owned by a foreign person is treated as a corporation for this reporting purpose, even though it is otherwise ignored for U.S. income tax. That single change is why so many international founders, who were told their LLC "doesn't file anything", in fact have a filing every year.

Does Form 5472 apply to you?

The answer depends on how your U.S. company is owned and taxed:

Your structureForm 5472?What you file
Single-member LLC owned by one non-U.S. person or foreign companyYesForm 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120
LLC or corporation taxed as a corporation, at least 25% foreign-ownedYes, if it has related-party transactionsForm 5472 attached to its full Form 1120
Multi-member LLC taxed as a partnershipGenerally noForm 1065, with its own foreign-partner rules

This guide is written mainly for the first row: one founder living in Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, the Gulf, India, or elsewhere, who formed a Wyoming, Delaware, or New Mexico LLC to sell online, invoice clients, or open a U.S. bank or payment account. If that describes you, the rest of this guide applies directly. Not sure which return your company files? See Form 1120 vs. Form 1065, or answer seven questions in our free Form 5472 checker.

What counts as a reportable transaction?

For a foreign-owned LLC, the definition is deliberately wide. The IRS instructions say it includes amounts paid or received in connection with the formation, dissolution, acquisition, and disposition of the company, as well as contributions and distributions. In practice:

TransactionReportable?
You paid the state filing fee or formation agent personallyYes
You sent money from your personal account to fund the LLC's bank accountYes
You transferred profits from the LLC to yourselfYes
You lent money to the LLC, or the LLC lent money to youYes
You paid an LLC subscription, software bill, or registered agent fee from a personal cardYes
The LLC received payments from unrelated customersNot on Form 5472, because customers are not related parties

"My LLC had no income. Do I still file?" Almost always, yes. Paying to form the company is itself a reportable transaction, so an LLC with no sales at all usually still has a filing in its first year.

Inside the form: what each part asks

Form 5472 is a three-page form. A foreign-owned single-member LLC typically completes these parts:

  1. Part I: the reporting corporationThe LLC's name, address, EIN, country of incorporation, and total assets. Line 3 is checked to show it is a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity.
  2. Part II: the 25% foreign shareholderFor an LLC, this is the foreign owner: name, address, country of citizenship and residence, and foreign tax number. If the owner has no foreign tax number, the instructions say to enter "None" or "N/A".
  3. Part III: the related partyThe person the LLC transacted with. For a single-owner LLC this is usually the same owner, described again with their relationship to the company.
  4. Part IV: monetary transactionsTotals for amounts such as loans, payments for services, and interest, where they apply. Amounts of $50,000 or less may be reported as "$50,000 or less", and reasonable estimates are allowed where exact figures cannot be determined.
  5. Part V: other transactionsWhere foreign-owned LLCs report contributions, distributions, and formation costs, with a short attached statement describing them.
  6. Part VI: nonmonetary and less-than-full consideration transactionsDealings with a foreign related party, such as the owner, where something other than money changed hands, or where the LLC paid or received less than full value. If there were any, the box is checked and a schedule is attached that describes the property, rights, obligations, or services exchanged and gives a reasonable estimate of their fair market value or, if that is not possible, another reasonable indicator of value. The instructions do not exempt foreign-owned LLCs from this part. If there were no such transactions, the box is left unchecked.
  7. Part VII: additional informationA short set of yes-or-no questions that all filers complete, except certain lines the instructions tell foreign-owned LLCs to skip.

A separate Form 5472 is required for each related party the company transacted with. For a typical single-owner LLC, that means one form.

The pro forma Form 1120

A foreign-owned LLC does not file a normal corporate tax return. It attaches Form 5472 to a pro forma Form 1120, which carries only the company's name, address, EIN, and a few basic items. The IRS instructions require the words "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" to be written across the top. Filing a pro forma return does not mean the LLC owes U.S. corporate tax. It is simply the cover the information return is attached to.

How and where to file

A foreign-owned LLC cannot file Form 5472 electronically. The return is faxed to 855-887-7737 or mailed to the dedicated IRS address given in the current instructions: Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd., M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201. The IRS asks for a fax resolution of 300 DPI or higher. These filers do not use the ordinary Form 1120 address. Keep the fax confirmation or postal receipt: it is your proof of the filing date.

Your LLC also needs an EIN to file. If it does not have one yet, apply early, because the process for owners outside the U.S. can take several weeks.

Deadlines

For calendar-year LLCs, the return is due on April 15 of the following year. The return for the 2026 tax year is due Thursday, April 15, 2027. Filing Form 7004 by that date moves the deadline to Friday, October 15, 2027. The first year runs from the formation date to December 31. See our Form 5472 deadline guide and how to file Form 7004.

Penalties, and why the year stays open

The penalty for a late, missing, or substantially incomplete Form 5472 is $25,000 per form, per year. If the failure continues more than 90 days after the IRS notifies you, a further $25,000 can apply for each 30-day period, with no maximum. Failing to keep adequate records can trigger the same penalty.

There is a second, less obvious consequence. When required information is not filed, the IRS's normal time limit for assessing tax on that year generally does not start to run. The year stays open until the information is provided. Read how Form 5472 penalty abatement works.

Documents to prepare

  • Articles of Organization, with the formation date and state
  • The IRS EIN confirmation letter
  • The owner's full name, address, country of citizenship and residence, and foreign tax number, if any
  • All of the year's statements for the LLC's bank and payment accounts
  • A dated list of money paid into, and taken out of, the company
  • Receipts for company costs you paid personally, including formation fees

The company must also keep records that support the figures reported. Organised records make the filing faster and protect you if the IRS ever asks questions.

Six common Form 5472 mistakes

  1. Assuming no income means no filingThe filing depends on related-party transactions, not profit.
  2. Leaving out formation costsFees you paid personally to set up the LLC are reportable, often making the first year the one owners miss.
  3. E-filing, or using the wrong addressForeign-owned LLCs fax or mail to a dedicated unit. A return sent elsewhere can be treated as not filed on time.
  4. Forgetting the first, short yearAn LLC formed in November still has a return for that year.
  5. Details that do not match IRS recordsThe LLC's name, EIN, and address should match the EIN letter exactly.
  6. Waiting after a missed yearFiling before the IRS contacts you is generally the strongest position. See what to do after a missed deadline.

Frequently asked questions

What is Form 5472 used for?

Form 5472 is an IRS information return that reports transactions between a U.S. company and its foreign owner or other foreign related parties. For a foreign-owned single-member LLC it is filed each year with a pro forma Form 1120, and it does not calculate any tax.

Do I have to file Form 5472 if my LLC had no income?

Usually, yes. The requirement depends on transactions between you and the company, such as paying formation fees or funding the bank account, not on whether the LLC earned money.

Can a foreign-owned LLC e-file Form 5472?

No. The IRS instructions state that a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity cannot file Form 5472 electronically. It is faxed to 855-887-7737 or mailed to the dedicated Ogden, Utah address.

Does a multi-member LLC file Form 5472?

Generally not. A multi-member LLC is normally taxed as a partnership and files Form 1065, which has its own rules for foreign partners. Form 5472 applies to single-member foreign-owned LLCs and to corporations that are at least 25% foreign-owned.

What is the penalty for not filing Form 5472?

$25,000 per form, per tax year, with a further $25,000 for each 30-day period the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notice. Relief may be available where there is reasonable cause.
