Do U.S. Owners Need Form 5472? Who Files and Who Does Not

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated November 1, 20265 min read

Form 5472 is triggered by foreign ownership of a U.S. company, not by where you live. A U.S. citizen running an LLC from Lagos does not file it. A U.S. corporation with a 25% foreign shareholder usually does. This guide shows exactly which businesses file, which do not, and why.

Key facts

  • U.S. citizens and green card holders are U.S. persons. An LLC they own alone is not foreign-owned and does not file Form 5472.
  • A U.S. corporation that is at least 25% foreign-owned files Form 5472 for each related party it has reportable transactions with, attached to its regular Form 1120.
  • A single-member LLC owned by a non-U.S. person files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year.
  • The penalty for a late or substantially incomplete Form 5472 is $25,000 per form, per year.

Who must file Form 5472

The IRS calls a company that must file a "reporting corporation". There are three types:

  • A U.S. corporation that is 25% or more foreign-owned. If a foreign person owns at least 25% of its voting power or value at any time during the year, and it has reportable transactions with a related party, it files Form 5472.
  • A foreign corporation engaged in a U.S. trade or business. It files Form 5472 with its U.S. return.
  • A foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity. A single-member LLC owned by a non-U.S. person files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, even though it normally pays no U.S. tax.

Moving abroad does not make you a foreign owner. A U.S. citizen remains a U.S. person wherever they live. Their LLC does not file Form 5472, but they still report its income on their own U.S. return and may need an FBAR for foreign accounts.

How it works for 25% foreign-owned U.S. corporations

  • One form per related party. A separate Form 5472 is filed for each foreign or U.S. related party the corporation dealt with during the year.
  • Reportable transactions include sales and purchases, services, rents and royalties, loans and interest, and other amounts paid to or received from the related party.
  • Filed with the tax return. Form 5472 is attached to the corporation's Form 1120 and shares its due date: the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end, April 15 for calendar-year corporations, or October 15 with Form 7004.
  • Records. The corporation must keep records that support the amounts it reports.

How it works for foreign-owned single-member LLCs

A single-member LLC owned by a non-U.S. person is treated as a corporation for this reporting only. It files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, completing only basic company details, and sends it to the IRS by fax or mail. For the full rules, read Form 5472 instructions explained, and for this year's dates see Form 5472 deadline 2026 and 2027.

Frequently asked questions

I am a U.S. citizen living in Nigeria with a Wyoming LLC. Do I file Form 5472?

No. As a U.S. citizen you are a U.S. person, so your LLC is not foreign-owned. You report its income on your Form 1040, and other filings such as an FBAR may apply.

My U.S. corporation has a foreign shareholder with 20%. Does it file?

Not on that basis alone. The test is 25% foreign ownership, by vote or value, at any time during the year, taking into account certain ownership attributed from related persons.

Can a 25% foreign-owned corporation e-file Form 5472?

Generally, yes, as part of its electronically filed Form 1120. The fax-or-mail rule applies to foreign-owned disregarded entities filing a pro forma Form 1120.
