U.S. LLC Taxes for Filipino Founders: A Yearly Filing Guide

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 21, 20266 min read

Key points

  • A U.S. LLC with one owner who lives in the Philippines generally must file Form 5472 and a pro forma Form 1120 each year, regardless of profit.
  • The filing is due on April 15 for calendar-year LLCs, or October 15 if Form 7004 is filed by April 15.
  • A missed or substantially incomplete Form 5472 can lead to a $25,000 penalty per form, per year.
  • The Philippines and the United States have an income tax treaty. It can affect U.S. tax and withholding, but it does not replace the Form 5472 filing.
  • Filipinos living abroad, including OFWs, are taxed differently at home from those who live in the Philippines, which matters if you run your LLC from overseas.

The Philippines is home to a large community of virtual assistants, developers, designers, bookkeepers, and online sellers who work with clients in the United States and around the world. Many of them, as well as Filipino professionals working overseas, now operate through a U.S. LLC to sign contracts directly, receive dollar payments, and access U.S. payment and e-commerce platforms. The company comes with a federal reporting obligation that is easy to miss and expensive to ignore. This guide explains it, along with the treaty position and the practical points for owners in Manila, Cebu, Davao, and beyond.

The yearly IRS requirement

A single-member LLC whose owner is not a U.S. person is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. It pays no U.S. income tax as an entity, but it is treated as a corporation for information reporting and must file Form 5472, attached to a pro forma Form 1120, to disclose its transactions with you.

The return is filed under the LLC's EIN and is sent to the IRS by fax or mail, as e-filing is not available for it. See Form 5472 Explained for the full picture and Form 5472 tax years and due dates for your deadlines.

Common reportable transactions for Filipino owners

Every transfer of money or value between you and the company is reportable. Typical examples:

  • Formation and setup costs paid from your personal bank account, card, or e-wallet before the LLC's own account was ready.
  • Transfers to fund the LLC, for example to pay for software, advertising, or inventory.
  • Withdrawals to the Philippines through a bank, payment service, or e-wallet. Each transfer from the LLC to you is a distribution.
  • Client payments received personally for work invoiced by the LLC, or LLC funds used for personal costs.

Note the dollar value of each transfer. If you convert funds to pesos through an e-wallet or remittance service, record the U.S. dollar amount and date at the time. It makes the annual filing far quicker and supports your Philippine records.

How the Philippines–U.S. tax treaty applies

The tax treaty between the Philippines and the United States determines which country may tax particular income. For an LLC owner who is resident in the Philippines:

  • Business profits are generally taxable in the U.S. only where they are attributable to a permanent establishment in the United States. Running the business from the Philippines without a U.S. office or dependent agent usually means there is none.
  • Reduced withholding may be available on certain U.S.-source income, such as dividends and royalties, if claimed in Part II of Form W-8BEN.
  • Treaty disclosure. If you are engaged in a U.S. trade or business but rely on the treaty to exempt the income, a Form 1040-NR with Form 8833 may be required.

Treaty benefits relate to tax. They do not remove the Form 5472 requirement.

The correct tax form for U.S. clients

U.S. clients and platforms usually ask for a tax form before paying. As the Filipino owner of a disregarded LLC, you generally provide Form W-8BEN in your own name as an individual, and you may claim treaty benefits in Part II where they apply. Form W-9 is for U.S. persons only.

Practical points for owners in the Philippines

  • EIN. Owners without an SSN generally obtain the LLC's EIN using Form SS-4, by fax or mail, or by telephone through the IRS line for international applicants.
  • Your Philippine TIN. Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign tax identification number. Use the TIN issued by the Bureau of Internal Revenue.
  • Time difference. The Philippines is twelve to thirteen hours ahead of U.S. Eastern time, so the U.S. deadline falls on what is already the next day in Manila. Plan to file early.
  • No U.S. visa, SSN, or ITIN is required to file Form 5472 for your LLC.

Other U.S. reporting

  • FBAR. A U.S. LLC counts as a U.S. person for FBAR purposes. If it has accounts outside the United States, for example a Philippine account in the company's name, with more than $10,000 in total at any time in the year, FinCEN Form 114 is required.
  • BOI. At the time of writing, companies formed in the United States are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting.

The Philippine side

Under Philippine tax rules, a resident citizen is generally taxed on worldwide income, which can include profits earned through a U.S. LLC. A non-resident citizen, such as a Filipino who lives and works abroad for most of the year, is generally taxed only on income from Philippine sources. If you are an OFW running a U.S. LLC from another country, your Philippine position may therefore differ from that of an owner based in the Philippines, and you should also consider the rules of the country where you live. Confirm the treatment with a Philippine tax adviser or the Bureau of Internal Revenue. Meritline Tax prepares the U.S. filings.

Missed filings

If your LLC has not filed Form 5472 for earlier years, each year is a separate late return. Filing every outstanding year promptly, before IRS contact, and with a reasonable cause statement where justified, generally gives you the best position. Read what to do after a missed Form 5472 deadline or see our late filings service.

Annual document list

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN letter
  • Your passport, current address, and Philippine TIN
  • Statements for all LLC bank, payment, and platform accounts
  • A dated log of transfers between you and the LLC, including e-wallet transfers
  • Receipts for company expenses paid personally

Questions Filipino owners ask

Does the Philippines–U.S. treaty remove the Form 5472 filing?

No. The treaty concerns how income is taxed. Form 5472 is an information return that a foreign-owned U.S. LLC with reportable transactions must still file each year.

I am an OFW and run my LLC from abroad. What changes?

The U.S. filing is the same. Your Philippine tax position may differ, because non-resident citizens are generally taxed only on Philippine-source income, and the country where you live may have its own rules.

Which tax number do I use on Form 5472?

Your Philippine TIN issued by the Bureau of Internal Revenue, entered as the owner's foreign tax identification number.

Do I file if my LLC made nothing?

Usually, yes. Funding the company or paying its setup costs yourself generally creates a filing requirement.

Can I handle this from the Philippines?

Yes. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS, and you can share documents with us online.
