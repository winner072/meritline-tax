U.S. LLC Taxes for South African Founders: Filings and the Tax Treaty

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated November 8, 20268 min read

Key points

  • A U.S. LLC owned solely by a South African resident generally must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, regardless of profit.
  • The U.S. return follows the LLC's U.S. tax year, normally the calendar year, and is due April 15 (October 15 with Form 7004). South Africa's March–February tax year does not change this.
  • The penalty for a late or substantially incomplete Form 5472 is $25,000 per form, per year.
  • South Africa and the United States have an income tax treaty, which can reduce U.S. tax and withholding but does not remove Form 5472.
  • South African exchange control rules and the tax treatment of foreign companies are important considerations for owners at home.

South African entrepreneurs have long been active in international markets, and a U.S. LLC is now a common vehicle for SaaS founders, agencies, consultants, and e-commerce sellers in Cape Town, Johannesburg, Durban, and beyond. It provides access to U.S. clients, payment processors, and marketplaces, and allows invoicing in dollars. It also comes with a U.S. information return that is frequently overlooked, and South African owners have additional local rules to consider when money and ownership cross borders. This guide covers both.

The annual IRS filing

A U.S. LLC with one owner who is not a U.S. person is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. It is disregarded for U.S. income tax but treated as a corporation for information reporting, so it must file Form 5472, attached to a pro forma Form 1120, each year to report its transactions with you.

The return is filed under the LLC's EIN and is faxed or mailed to the IRS; it cannot be e-filed. See Form 5472 Explained for the full detail.

Two tax years to track

South Africa's tax year for individuals runs from March 1 to the end of February. Your U.S. LLC generally uses its owner's U.S. tax year, which for most South African owners without a U.S. filing requirement is the calendar year. Form 5472 therefore covers January to December, while your SARS return covers March to February. Keep monthly records so that figures can be prepared for both periods. See Form 5472 tax years and due dates for the U.S. rules.

Reportable transactions for South African owners

Every transfer of money or value between you and the LLC is reportable. Common examples include:

  • Setup costs paid personally, such as formation and registered agent fees charged to a South African card.
  • Funding the LLC by sending rand converted to dollars from your personal account.
  • Distributions when profit is transferred from the LLC to your personal account in South Africa.
  • Loans between you and the company, in either direction.

Funding a foreign company is also an exchange control matter. Sending money offshore to capitalize a U.S. LLC typically involves your bank as an authorised dealer, and may fall within your foreign investment allowances or require approval, depending on the amounts. Keep the bank documentation for each transfer: it supports both your exchange control position and your Form 5472.

The South Africa–U.S. tax treaty

The tax treaty between South Africa and the United States determines which country may tax particular income and how double taxation is relieved. For a South African-resident LLC owner:

  • Business profits are generally taxable in the U.S. only if they are attributable to a permanent establishment there. Running the business from South Africa, without a U.S. office or dependent agent, usually means there is none.
  • Reduced withholding on certain U.S.-source income, such as dividends and royalties, may be available when claimed in Part II of Form W-8BEN.
  • Treaty disclosure. If you are engaged in a U.S. trade or business but rely on the treaty to avoid U.S. tax, a Form 1040-NR with Form 8833 may be required.
  • Limitation on benefits. Like other modern U.S. treaties, the South African treaty includes rules restricting who can claim its benefits; individuals resident in South Africa generally qualify.

The treaty does not remove Form 5472, which is an information return that applies regardless of tax.

Forms for U.S. clients and platforms

As the South African owner of a disregarded LLC, you generally provide U.S. clients and platforms with Form W-8BEN in your own name, claiming treaty benefits in Part II where they apply. Form W-9 is only for U.S. persons.

Practical points when filing from South Africa

  • EIN. Owners without an SSN usually obtain the LLC's EIN through Form SS-4, by fax or mail, or by telephone through the IRS international line.
  • Time difference. South Africa is six to seven hours ahead of U.S. Eastern time. Plan to send the return several days before the deadline.
  • No travel required. You do not need a U.S. visa, SSN, or ITIN to file Form 5472 for your LLC.

Your SARS number on Form 5472

Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign taxpayer identification number (FTIN) on line 4b(3), and again on line 8b(3), because a single owner is usually also the related party. For a South African resident, this is your SARS income tax reference number, the 10-digit number shown on your SARS correspondence and eFiling profile.

No tax number? Do not leave the box empty. The IRS instructions say to write "None" or "N/A" when the owner has no FTIN, and to use the same number consistently every year. See exactly where it goes in our completed Form 5472 example.

Your tax number depends on where you are tax resident. See our guides for Egypt and Kenya, or compare all 14 countries.

Other U.S. reporting

  • FBAR. A U.S. LLC is a U.S. person for FBAR. If it holds accounts outside the United States, such as a South African account in its name, totalling more than $10,000 at any time in the year, FinCEN Form 114 is due.
  • BOI. At the time of writing, companies formed in the United States are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting.

The South African side

South African tax residents are taxed on their worldwide income. You are resident if you are ordinarily resident in South Africa, or if you meet the physical presence test, which looks at days spent in South Africa in the current year and the preceding five years. Because a U.S. LLC may be treated as a foreign company for South African purposes, the rules on controlled foreign companies may also be relevant, and any income taxed in both countries can be relieved under the treaty. These questions, together with exchange control, should be confirmed with a South African tax adviser. Meritline Tax prepares the U.S. filings.

Missed years

Each year in which Form 5472 should have been filed but was not is a separate late return. Filing every outstanding year promptly, before IRS contact, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support it, is generally the best course. See what to do after a missed Form 5472 deadline and our catch-up service.

Records to keep

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN letter
  • Your passport, South African address, and SARS tax reference number
  • Statements for every LLC bank, payment, and marketplace account, kept monthly
  • Bank documentation for each offshore transfer to or from the LLC
  • Receipts for company costs you paid personally

Questions South African owners ask

Does the South Africa–U.S. tax treaty remove the Form 5472 filing?

No. The treaty concerns taxation. Form 5472 is an information return that a foreign-owned U.S. LLC with reportable transactions must file each year.

My SARS year ends in February. Which period does Form 5472 cover?

The LLC's U.S. tax year, which for most South African owners is the calendar year, January to December.

Do I need exchange control approval to fund my LLC?

It depends on the amounts and on your allowances. Transfers are usually handled through your bank as an authorised dealer. Confirm the requirements with your bank or adviser before sending funds.

Does an LLC with no profit still file?

Usually, yes. Paying the company's costs or funding its account yourself generally creates reportable transactions.

Can I file from South Africa?

Yes. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS, and documents can be shared with us online.
