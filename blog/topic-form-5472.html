Form 5472 and Pro Forma 1120: Guides for Foreign-Owned U.S. LLCs

Updated November 10, 20269 guides

Form 5472 is the yearly IRS information return that a U.S. LLC owned by one foreign person files with a pro forma Form 1120. It reports the money that moved between the company and its owner, such as formation costs, capital paid in, and money taken out. It applies even when the LLC earned nothing, and a late or incomplete return carries a $25,000 penalty.

Who files Form 5472?

Two groups: U.S. LLCs wholly owned by one foreign person, which attach it to a pro forma Form 1120, and U.S. corporations that are at least 25% foreign-owned, which attach it to their full Form 1120. Multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships generally file Form 1065 instead, and U.S. owners generally do not file Form 5472 for their own LLC.

Frequently asked questions

What is Form 5472 used for?

Form 5472 reports transactions between a U.S. company and its foreign owner or other foreign related parties. For a foreign-owned single-member LLC it is filed every year with a pro forma Form 1120 and does not calculate tax.

Can a foreign-owned LLC e-file Form 5472?

No. The IRS instructions say a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity cannot file Form 5472 electronically. It is faxed or mailed to the dedicated IRS unit in Ogden, Utah.
