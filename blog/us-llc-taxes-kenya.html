U.S. LLC Taxes for Kenyan Founders: A Yearly Filing Guide

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated November 1, 20269 min read

Key points

  • A Kenyan resident who solely owns a U.S. LLC will generally need to file Form 5472 and a pro forma Form 1120 for the company each year, whether or not it made a profit.
  • The due date for calendar-year LLCs is April 15, extendable to October 15 with Form 7004.
  • Failure to file, or filing a substantially incomplete form, carries a $25,000 penalty per form, per year.
  • Kenya does not have an income tax treaty with the United States, so ordinary U.S. rules apply to withholding and to whether U.S. tax is owed.
  • Your KRA PIN is the Kenyan tax identification number to provide on Form 5472.

Kenya has one of the most active freelance and digital business communities in Africa. Developers, writers, designers, virtual assistants, and online sellers in Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, and beyond often set up a U.S. LLC to contract with international clients, receive payments in dollars, and access U.S. payment providers and marketplaces. Setting up the company is the easy part. Keeping it compliant requires an annual federal filing that formation services rarely emphasize, and the penalty for missing it is severe. This guide explains the U.S. obligations for Kenyan owners, the Kenya-specific issues worth knowing, and how to stay on track year after year.

The filing every Kenyan-owned LLC must make

For U.S. income tax purposes, a single-member LLC owned by a non-U.S. person is a disregarded entity. For information reporting, however, it is treated as a corporation, and it must disclose its transactions with its foreign owner on Form 5472, attached to a pro forma Form 1120.

This is a disclosure return rather than a tax return: it records what passed between you and the company during the year. It is filed under the LLC's EIN, is not accepted through standard e-filing, and is sent to the IRS by fax or post. See Form 5472 Explained for a full walkthrough, and Form 5472 tax years and due dates for help with your dates.

Typical reportable transactions for Kenyan owners

Any transfer of money or value between you and your LLC is reportable. For owners based in Kenya, these come up most often:

  • Start-up costs paid personally. Formation, registered agent, and first-year subscription costs paid from your personal bank card or dollar account are contributions to the LLC.
  • Payments routed through M-Pesa or linked wallets. If you move funds between the LLC's payment accounts and your personal M-Pesa or bank account, each movement is either a contribution or a distribution and should be recorded.
  • Profit withdrawals. Sending the LLC's earnings to your personal account in Kenya is a distribution to you.
  • Platform payouts received personally. If a client or platform pays you personally for work invoiced by the LLC, or the reverse, the resulting transfers between you and the company need to be accounted for.

Separate the company's money from your own. Paying the LLC's costs only from its own account, and moving profit out in clearly recorded transfers, makes Form 5472 far simpler to prepare and supports your Kenyan tax position too.

How the absence of a treaty affects your U.S. tax

Kenya and the United States have no income tax treaty in force. For a Kenyan owner, that means:

  • The U.S. trade or business test applies directly. Without treaty protection, U.S. tax can arise once your activities amount to a U.S. trade or business, rather than at the higher permanent establishment threshold found in treaties. Owners who work from Kenya, with no U.S. office, staff, agents acting for them in the U.S., or U.S. inventory, frequently owe no U.S. income tax, but the answer depends on your facts.
  • Withholding stays at the standard rate. Certain U.S.-source payments, such as dividends and royalties, are subject to 30% withholding with no reduction.
  • W-8BEN without a treaty claim. The treaty section of the form is left blank.

The Form 5472 requirement applies regardless of whether any U.S. tax is due.

The correct form for U.S. clients and platforms

Most U.S. clients, payment processors, and freelance platforms ask for a tax form before releasing payment. Because the LLC is disregarded and you are a non-U.S. individual, you generally complete Form W-8BEN in your own name. Form W-9 is reserved for U.S. persons; submitting one by mistake can cause your earnings to be reported to the IRS as U.S. income.

Practical points when filing from Kenya

  • Obtaining an EIN. Without a U.S. Social Security Number, the online EIN application is generally unavailable. Kenyan owners typically apply using Form SS-4 by fax or mail, or by calling the IRS line for international applicants.
  • Submitting the return. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS. An online fax service works well from Kenya; keep the confirmation of successful transmission.
  • Time difference. Kenya is seven to eight hours ahead of U.S. Eastern time, depending on U.S. daylight saving. A deadline that has not yet passed in the U.S. may already feel like the next day in Nairobi, so plan to file early.
  • No U.S. visit, SSN, or ITIN required to file Form 5472 for your LLC.

Your KRA PIN on Form 5472

Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign taxpayer identification number (FTIN) on line 4b(3), and again on line 8b(3), because a single owner is usually also the related party. For a Kenyan resident, this is your KRA PIN from the Kenya Revenue Authority: 11 characters, starting and ending with a letter.

No tax number? Do not leave the box empty. The IRS instructions say to write "None" or "N/A" when the owner has no FTIN, and to use the same number consistently every year. See exactly where it goes in our completed Form 5472 example.

Your tax number depends on where you are tax resident. See our guides for South Africa and Turkey, or compare all 14 countries.

State obligations

Each state has its own annual requirements for LLCs formed there:

StateAnnual requirement
WyomingAnnual report and license tax, due on the first day of the month in which the LLC was formed.
DelawareAnnual LLC tax, due June 1 each year.
New MexicoNo annual report required for LLCs; maintain a registered agent.

Late state filings attract fees and can lead to the company being administratively dissolved. See our state filings guide.

Additional reporting

  • FBAR. A U.S. LLC counts as a U.S. person for FBAR purposes. If it has accounts outside the United States, for example a Kenyan bank account in the LLC's name, with balances totalling more than $10,000 at any point in the year, FinCEN Form 114 is required.
  • BOI. At the time of writing, companies created in the United States are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting. This does not change the Form 5472 requirement.

Tax in Kenya

You are generally a Kenyan tax resident if you have a permanent home in Kenya and were present there during the year, or if you spent 183 days or more in Kenya in the year, or an average of more than 122 days a year across that year and the two before it. Kenya's Income Tax Act can treat profits from a business carried on by a Kenyan resident, even partly outside Kenya, as income taxable in Kenya. If your LLC sells digital services to customers in Kenya, Kenya's significant economic presence tax rules may also be relevant.

These are matters for the Kenya Revenue Authority, so confirm how your LLC income should be reported with a Kenyan tax adviser. Meritline Tax prepares the U.S. filings; the transaction records you keep for Form 5472 will also support your Kenyan returns.

Behind on your filings?

Many Kenyan owners discover Form 5472 only after a year or more of operating. Every unfiled year is its own late return. Bringing all outstanding years up to date at once, before any IRS contact, and supporting the filings with a well-documented reasonable cause statement where appropriate, is generally the most effective approach. Read what to do if you missed a Form 5472 deadline, or see our late filings and catch-up service.

Annual document checklist

  • Certificate or Articles of Organization and your EIN confirmation letter
  • Your passport details, Kenyan address, and KRA PIN
  • Statements for all LLC bank, payment, and platform accounts
  • A dated record of transfers between you and the LLC, including through M-Pesa
  • Receipts for any company expenses you paid personally

Questions Kenyan owners often ask

Which Kenyan number goes on Form 5472?

Your Kenya Revenue Authority PIN, entered as the owner's foreign taxpayer identification number.

Is there a U.S.–Kenya tax treaty?

No. With no income tax treaty in force, ordinary U.S. rules apply, including standard 30% withholding on certain U.S.-source payments and no treaty claim on Form W-8BEN.

My LLC had no income. Is Form 5472 still required?

In most cases, yes. Funding the company or paying its formation and running costs yourself creates reportable transactions, regardless of profit.

Do M-Pesa transfers need to be reported?

Transfers between you and the LLC are reportable however they are made, including through M-Pesa or linked wallets. Keep records showing the date, amount, and purpose of each one.

Can the whole process be handled from Kenya?

Yes. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS, and you can send your documents to us online without travelling.
