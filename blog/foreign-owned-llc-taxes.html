Foreign-Owned LLC Taxes: The Complete Guide for Non-U.S. Owners

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated November 10, 20269 min read

A U.S. LLC owned by one person outside the United States usually owes no U.S. income tax, but it always has U.S. tax filings. The difference between those two facts is where most foreign owners go wrong. This guide brings the whole picture into one place: how the IRS sees your LLC, the yearly federal return, when U.S. tax does apply, the other forms you may meet, state obligations, your home country, and a calendar for the year.

Key points

  • The yearly federal filing for a foreign-owned single-member LLC is Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, due April 15, even with no income.
  • U.S. income tax generally applies only to income connected with a U.S. trade or business, or to certain U.S.-source passive income.
  • The state where the LLC was formed has its own annual report or tax, separate from the IRS.
  • Your home country may tax the profits, and may see the LLC differently from the IRS.

How the IRS sees a foreign-owned LLC

An LLC is a state-law company, and the IRS decides how to tax it based on its owners and any election it makes:

OwnershipDefault U.S. tax treatmentMain federal return
One foreign ownerDisregarded entity, treated as a corporation for Form 5472 reportingForm 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120
Two or more ownersPartnershipForm 1065, with Schedules K-1 and partnership withholding rules for foreign partners
Any LLC that elects corporate statusC corporation, taxed at 21% on its own profitsForm 1120, with Form 5472 where it has a 25% foreign owner

Most foreign founders run a single-member LLC, so the rest of this guide focuses on that structure. If you have a partner, read Form 1120 vs Form 1065.

The yearly federal filing: Form 5472 and the pro forma 1120

Since 2017, a single-member LLC wholly owned by a foreign person must file Form 5472 every year it has reportable transactions with its owner or other related parties. That includes money you put in, money you take out, formation costs you paid personally, loans, and payments for services between you and the company. In practice, almost every active LLC has at least one.

  • How it is filed: attached to a pro forma Form 1120 that shows only the LLC's name, address, EIN, and item E, with "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top.
  • Where it goes: by fax to 855-887-7737 or by mail to the dedicated IRS unit in Ogden, Utah. It cannot be e-filed.
  • When: April 15 for calendar-year LLCs, or October 15 with a Form 7004 extension filed by April 15.
  • The penalty: $25,000 per form, per year, for a late, missing, or substantially incomplete return.

Do you owe U.S. income tax?

Form 5472 is an information return; it calculates no tax. Whether U.S. income tax is due is a separate question, and for a disregarded LLC it is answered at the level of the owner. A non-resident owner is generally taxed by the U.S. in only two situations:

  1. Income effectively connected with a U.S. trade or businessThis usually requires real activity in the United States, such as a U.S. office, U.S. employees, or dependent agents acting for the business there, or in some cases inventory held in U.S. warehouses. Such income is taxed at normal U.S. rates, and the owner files a U.S. return, usually Form 1040-NR.
  2. Certain U.S.-source passive incomeItems such as U.S. dividends, interest, rents, and royalties can be subject to 30% withholding, which a tax treaty may reduce.

A founder who runs the business entirely from outside the United States, selling services or digital products performed abroad, often has no U.S. income tax to pay. Where a tax treaty applies, business profits are generally taxable in the U.S. only if there is a permanent establishment there. These are fact-based tests, so review them whenever your setup changes, for example if you hire in the U.S. or start using U.S. fulfilment centres.

No tax due never means no filing due. Form 5472 is required whether or not the LLC owes any U.S. tax.

Beneficial ownership reporting to FinCEN under the Corporate Transparency Act does not apply to companies formed in the United States. FinCEN made this exemption permanent in a final rule effective August 14, 2026. Form 5472 is a separate IRS filing and is still due.

State taxes and annual reports

Your LLC also answers to the state where it was formed. These obligations are separate from the IRS and continue whether or not the LLC earns anything:

StateYearly obligation
Delaware$400 annual LLC tax, due June 1
WyomingAnnual report, from $60, due by the first day of the anniversary month
FloridaAnnual report, $138.75, due between January 1 and May 1
New MexicoNo annual report for LLCs

Every state also requires a registered agent. Missing a state filing can lead to penalties and, eventually, the loss of good standing. See our state filings service.

Your home country

Most countries tax their residents on worldwide income, so profits that the U.S. does not tax may still be taxable at home. Your home tax authority may also treat the LLC as a separate foreign company rather than "disregarded", which changes how its profits and the money you take out are taxed. Some countries add foreign asset reporting or foreign exchange rules. Our country guides explain the U.S. side for owners in 14 countries, including which countries have a U.S. tax treaty and the tax number each owner enters on Form 5472. For the home-country return, work with a local adviser.

Penalties, and how to stay safe

The Form 5472 penalty is $25,000 per form, per year, and a further $25,000 for each 30 days a failure continues more than 90 days after an IRS notice. It applies whether or not any tax was due. The safest habits are simple: file every year, keep records of each owner transaction, and file any missed year before the IRS writes to you. Frequently asked questions

Does a foreign-owned LLC pay U.S. taxes?

Often not. A single-member LLC run entirely from outside the United States usually owes no U.S. income tax, because U.S. tax generally applies only to income connected with a U.S. trade or business or to certain U.S.-source passive income. It still has to file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year.

What tax forms does a foreign-owned single-member LLC file?

Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120 every year, plus Form 7004 if an extension is needed. The owner gives U.S. payers Form W-8BEN, and files Form 1040-NR only if there is U.S. taxable income. The state of formation has its own annual report or tax.

Do I need an ITIN or SSN for my LLC's taxes?

Not for Form 5472. The LLC needs an EIN, and a foreign owner without an SSN or ITIN is identified on Form 5472 with a reference ID number and their foreign tax number. An ITIN may be needed if the owner must file a U.S. income tax return.

What if my LLC had no income this year?

The Form 5472 filing usually still applies, because it depends on transactions between you and the company, such as funding the account or paying formation fees, not on profit.

Can my home country tax my LLC's profits?

Yes, in many cases. Most countries tax residents on worldwide income, and some treat a U.S. LLC as a separate foreign company. A tax treaty, where one exists, helps decide which country taxes what.
