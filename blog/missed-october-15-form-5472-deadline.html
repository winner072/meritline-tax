Missed the October 15 Form 5472 Deadline? What Foreign-Owned LLCs Should Do Now

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorOctober 20, 20267 min read

Key points

  • For calendar-year LLCs that filed Form 7004, October 15, 2026 was the final deadline for the 2025 Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120. No further extension is available.
  • If no extension was filed, the return was due on April 15, 2026, and it is already late.
  • The penalty for a late or substantially incomplete Form 5472 is $25,000 per form, per year, with further penalties if the failure continues after an IRS notice.
  • The most effective step is to file a complete, accurate return as soon as possible, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one.
  • Filing before the IRS contacts you generally puts you in a stronger position than waiting.

October 15 is the last date many foreign owners of U.S. LLCs can file their Form 5472 for the previous year. If that date has passed and your return has not been sent, you are far from alone, and the situation is usually manageable. What matters now is acting promptly and filing correctly. This guide explains where you stand, what the penalty exposure looks like, and the steps that lead back to full compliance.

Which deadline did you miss?

For a calendar-year, foreign-owned single-member LLC, the 2025 return had two possible deadlines:

Your situationDeadline that appliedStatus today
Form 7004 extension filed by April 15, 2026Thursday, October 15, 2026Late since October 16, 2026
No extension filedWednesday, April 15, 2026Late since April 16, 2026
Earlier years never filedApril 15 of each following yearEach year is a separate late filing

An extension cannot be requested after its deadline, and there is no second extension beyond October 15. From this point, the only route to compliance is a late filing. If you are unsure which tax year or dates apply to your company, see our guide to Form 5472 tax years and due dates.

What happens when Form 5472 is filed late

  • The initial penalty. The IRS can assess a penalty of $25,000 for each Form 5472 that is not filed when due. Filing a substantially incomplete form is treated the same as not filing.
  • Continuation penalties. If the failure continues for more than 90 days after the IRS sends a notice, an additional $25,000 can apply for each 30-day period, or part of a period, that it continues.
  • The year stays open. When required information is not filed, the IRS's normal time limit for assessing tax for that year generally does not begin to run.

In practice, penalties for late international information returns are frequently assessed when the late return is processed, and they are typically communicated by an IRS notice. A penalty notice is not the end of the matter: relief can be requested where there is reasonable cause, as explained below.

Why not simply wait? Waiting does not make the obligation disappear. It leaves the year open, risks continuation penalties once the IRS makes contact, and weakens the case for relief. Filing on your own initiative, before any IRS contact, is generally viewed more favorably.

Steps to get back into compliance

  1. Confirm the tax year and the period coveredMost foreign-owned LLCs report on a calendar-year basis. Your first year begins on the date the LLC was formed.
  2. Gather your records for the yearBank statements, formation documents, your EIN letter, and records of every transaction between you and the company, including capital you contributed, money you withdrew, and company costs you paid personally.
  3. Prepare a complete pro forma Form 1120 and Form 5472Write "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" across the top of the Form 1120, and complete every required item on Form 5472. Accuracy matters more than speed: a rushed, incomplete form can itself be treated as a failure to file.
  4. Attach a reasonable cause statement, where the facts support itExplain clearly and truthfully why the return was late and what you did once you became aware of the requirement.
  5. Send the return to the IRS and keep proofA foreign-owned LLC cannot e-file this return. It is faxed to 855-887-7737 or mailed to the IRS in Ogden, Utah, at the address given in the current Form 5472 instructions. Keep the fax confirmation or postal receipt.
  6. Check whether earlier years are also missingEach tax year is a separate filing obligation. If more than one year is outstanding, they should be addressed together.
  7. Respond promptly to any IRS correspondenceIf a notice arrives, read it carefully and respond within the time stated. Do not ignore it.

Understanding reasonable cause

The IRS may waive a Form 5472 penalty if the failure was due to reasonable cause and not wilful neglect. Reasonable cause generally means that you exercised ordinary business care and prudence but were still unable to file on time. The IRS considers all of the facts and circumstances, such as:

  • reliance on a professional adviser who failed to file or gave incorrect advice;
  • serious illness, a bereavement, or circumstances genuinely beyond your control;
  • the steps you took to understand your obligations, and how quickly you acted once you learned of them.

A reasonable cause statement must be in writing, set out the specific facts, and be signed under penalties of perjury. It should be factual, consistent with your records, and specific to your situation. Relief is not guaranteed, and simply not knowing about the requirement is rarely sufficient on its own. A carefully prepared statement, however, gives the IRS a clear basis on which to consider relief.

Already received an IRS penalty notice?

If the IRS has already assessed a penalty, the response becomes a request for abatement. Note the response date on the notice, gather your evidence, and submit a written reasonable cause explanation within that period. If the request is refused, you may have further appeal rights. Acting within the time limits on the notice is essential.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Filing an incomplete form simply to have something on record.
  • Waiting for the IRS to make contact before acting.
  • Filing only the most recent year when earlier years are also missing.
  • Trying to e-file, or sending the return to the general Form 1120 address.
  • Writing a vague or generic reasonable cause statement.
  • Ignoring IRS notices or responding after the deadline they set.

Plan ahead for the next deadline

Once your late return is filed, put the next deadline in your calendar. For calendar-year LLCs, the 2026 Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120 are due on Thursday, April 15, 2027, or Friday, October 15, 2027 if Form 7004 is filed by April 15. Keeping clean records through the year makes the next filing straightforward. Our Form 5472 deadline guide for 2026 and 2027 lists every date.
