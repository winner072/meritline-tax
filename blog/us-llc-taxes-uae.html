U.S.–UAE Tax Treaty: Is There One? Form 5472 and U.S. LLC Taxes for UAE Founders

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated November 8, 20268 min read

There is no income tax treaty between the United States and the United Arab Emirates. For a founder in Dubai or Abu Dhabi who owns a U.S. LLC, that single fact shapes how U.S. tax works: there is no treaty to lower U.S. withholding and no treaty "permanent establishment" protection. What does not change is the yearly filing. A U.S. LLC owned by one UAE resident almost always files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, every year, even when the UAE itself charges no personal income tax.

Key points

  • No U.S.–UAE income tax treaty. The two countries have a FATCA agreement on bank information, which is not a tax treaty.
  • Yearly filing: Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, due April 15 (or October 15 with Form 7004).
  • Penalty: $25,000 per form, per year, for a late or missing filing.
  • UAE side: no personal income tax, but UAE Corporate Tax can apply to individuals whose business turnover exceeds AED 1 million a year.

Who this guide is for

The UAE is home to a large international community: consultants, e-commerce sellers, agency owners, and remote founders of many nationalities who live in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, or Sharjah. Many form a Wyoming, Delaware, or New Mexico LLC to sell to American customers, use U.S. payment platforms, or hold a U.S. bank account. This guide applies if you are not a U.S. citizen or green card holder and you are the only owner of the LLC. If you are a U.S. citizen living in the UAE, the rules are different: see do U.S. owners need Form 5472?

The yearly federal filing

A U.S. LLC with one foreign owner is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. Since 2017, it must file Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120 to report transactions with its owner, such as formation costs, money paid in, and money taken out. The return calculates no tax. It is filed under the LLC's EIN by fax or mail, not e-file. Read Form 5472 explained, or check your situation with our free Form 5472 checker.

What "no tax treaty" means for you

Founders often assume that because the UAE has no personal income tax, nothing is owed anywhere. The U.S. side works differently:

U.S. tax on business profitsGenerally only with a U.S. permanent establishmentTaxable if the income is effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business, a broader test

Form 5472 filingRequiredRequired

In practice, a UAE-based consultant or agency owner who performs all the work from the UAE, with no U.S. office, staff, or inventory, usually has no effectively connected U.S. income and owes no U.S. income tax on those service fees. The risk rises when the business has U.S. employees or contractors acting for it, a U.S. office or warehouse, or inventory stored in the U.S., for example through Amazon FBA. Without a treaty, these facts are judged under U.S. domestic law alone, so they deserve careful review before you file.

Tax forms U.S. platforms will ask for

Because the LLC is disregarded and you are its foreign owner, U.S. clients and platforms will normally need Form W-8BEN in your own name, showing the UAE as your country of residence. Leave the treaty section blank, as there is no U.S.–UAE treaty to claim. Do not submit a Form W-9, which is for U.S. persons.

The UAE side

The UAE charges no personal income tax on salaries or investment income. Since June 2023, however, the UAE has had a federal Corporate Tax, generally 9% on taxable profits above AED 375,000. It can apply to individuals as well as companies: a natural person running a business in the UAE becomes subject to it once their business turnover exceeds AED 1 million in a calendar year. Questions about how a U.S. LLC managed from the UAE is treated, and whether registration with the Federal Tax Authority is needed, depend on your facts. We prepare U.S. filings only, so confirm the UAE position with a UAE tax adviser.

Your tax number on Form 5472 as a UAE resident

Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign taxpayer identification number (FTIN) on line 4b(3), and again on line 8b(3), because a single owner is usually also the related party. The UAE does not levy personal income tax, so most individuals have no tax identification number, and an Emirates ID number is not a tax number. If you are registered with the Federal Tax Authority and hold a Tax Registration Number (TRN), enter it. If not, write "None".

No tax number? Do not leave the box empty. The IRS instructions say to write "None" or "N/A" when the owner has no FTIN, and to use the same number consistently every year. See exactly where it goes in our completed Form 5472 example.

Your tax number depends on where you are tax resident. See our guides for Saudi Arabia, India, and Pakistan, or compare all 14 countries.

State filings

Your LLC also answers to its state. Wyoming requires an annual report by the first day of the month the company was formed. Delaware charges LLCs an annual tax due June 1. Florida's annual report is due between January 1 and May 1. New Mexico does not require an annual report from LLCs. See our state filings service.

Other U.S. reporting

  • FBAR. A U.S. LLC is treated as a U.S. person for FBAR. If it has accounts outside the United States, such as a UAE bank account in the company's name, totalling more than $10,000 at any point in the year, FinCEN Form 114 is due.
  • BOI. At the time of writing, companies formed in the United States are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting. This does not affect Form 5472.

If you have missed earlier years

Each year without a required Form 5472 is a separate late return. Filing all outstanding years together, before the IRS contacts you, and including a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one, generally gives the strongest position. Read how Form 5472 penalty abatement works or see our catch-up service.

What to keep ready each year

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN letter
  • Your passport details, UAE address, and Emirates ID or foreign tax number if any
  • All statements for the LLC's bank and payment accounts
  • A dated list of money you put in and took out
  • Details of any U.S. staff, contractors, office, or inventory

Frequently asked questions

Is there a tax treaty between the U.S. and the UAE?

No. The United States and the UAE do not have an income tax treaty. They have a FATCA intergovernmental agreement for sharing financial account information, which does not reduce U.S. tax or withholding.

The UAE has no income tax. Do I still file Form 5472?

Yes, in most cases. Form 5472 is a U.S. information return based on transactions between you and your LLC. It applies regardless of whether your country of residence taxes you.

Will I pay U.S. tax on my LLC's income?

Often not, if all work is done from the UAE and the business has no U.S. office, staff, or inventory. Income effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business is taxable, and without a treaty this is judged under U.S. law alone, so review your facts.

Should I claim treaty benefits on Form W-8BEN?

No. There is no U.S.–UAE treaty, so the treaty section is left blank. The form still confirms that you are not a U.S. person.
