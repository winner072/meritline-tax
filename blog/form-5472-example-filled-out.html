Form 5472 Example: A Completed Sample for a Foreign-Owned Single-Member LLC

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorOctober 31, 20269 min read

Most explanations of Form 5472 describe the form in words. This guide shows it completed. We follow one fictional, foreign-owned single-member LLC through its first tax year and fill in every part that applies: the pro forma Form 1120, Form 5472 Parts I to VII, and the Part V statement, with a short explanation of each entry. You can also download the whole sample as a PDF.

Key points

  • A complete package has three pieces: the pro forma Form 1120 on top, Form 5472 behind it, and a statement describing the Part V transactions.
  • Money you put in and take out is reportable. For a foreign-owned LLC, contributions, distributions and formation costs belong in Part V, described on a statement.
  • No ITIN is needed. An owner without a U.S. number uses a reference ID number and gives their foreign tax number, or writes "None".
  • Income from customers is not on Form 5472. The form reports transactions with the owner and other related parties only.

Part I describes the LLC itself. For a foreign-owned LLC, the IRS treats the LLC as the "reporting corporation" for this form only, even though it is disregarded for income tax.

  • 1cTotal assets at the end of the year. Here that is the $11,000 bank balance, as the LLC owns nothing else.
  • 1d–1eA short description of the business and the six-digit code from the list in the Form 1120 instructions. 541600 covers management consulting.

  • 2–3Both boxes are checked: a foreign person owns 100% of the LLC, and it is a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity.

  • 4b(1)Left blank, because James has no SSN or ITIN. An ITIN is not required to file.
  • 4b(2)A reference ID number the LLC creates itself: letters and numbers only, no spaces or symbols, up to 50 characters. No IRS approval is needed, but the same ID must be used every year.
  • 4b(3)The owner's foreign tax number, such as a UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). If the owner has none, write "None" or "N/A". Leaving it empty can make the form look incomplete.

Parts IV to VI: where the transactions go

This is the part most often filled in wrongly. Part IV covers ordinary commercial dealings between the LLC and its owner, such as sales, services, rent, loans and interest. Sample Consulting LLC had none of those with James, so Part IV is blank.

Part V is specific to foreign-owned LLCs. The instructions say it covers amounts paid or received in connection with forming, dissolving, acquiring or disposing of the entity, including contributions to and distributions from it. James's $300, $5,000 and $2,000 all belong here, so the box is checked and the details go on a statement.

Some preparers also show these amounts in Part IV. This sample follows the wording of the instructions, which place formation costs, contributions and distributions in Part V. If the owner had lent the LLC money or invoiced it for services, those amounts would go in Part IV.

The Part V statement

The statement is a plain page attached behind Form 5472. It should identify the LLC and the tax year, and describe each transaction clearly enough that the amounts can be traced to the bank statements.

Part VII: the additional questions

Every reporting corporation must answer Part VII. For a typical owner-operated LLC with no imports, loans or cost sharing, the answers are "No". Line 43 is completed by domestic corporations only, so a foreign-owned LLC leaves it blank.

The pro forma Form 1120 cover page

A foreign-owned LLC does not pay corporate tax, but Form 5472 has to travel attached to a Form 1120. The instructions say only the name, the address, item B (the EIN) and item E are required. "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" is written across the top, and the owner signs the return.

Putting the package together and sending it

  1. Order the pagesPro forma Form 1120 on top, Form 5472 behind it, then the Part V statement.
  2. Sign the Form 1120The owner signs and dates it. A paid preparer also signs the preparer section.
  3. Send it to the dedicated IRS unitFax to 855-887-7737 or mail to the Ogden, Utah address for foreign-owned U.S. DEs. These returns cannot be e-filed.
  4. Keep the proofSave the fax confirmation or the postal tracking, along with the bank statements behind every figure.
  5. Meet the dateFor calendar year 2026 the return is due April 15, 2027, or October 15, 2027 if Form 7004 is filed by April 15.

Adapting the sample to your own LLC

The structure stays the same for most single-member LLCs, but your entries will differ if any of these apply:

  • You lent money to the LLC, or it paid you for services or rent. Those amounts go in Part IV, not only Part V.
  • The LLC is owned through a foreign company. That company is the direct owner in Part II, and the person behind it may need to be reported as an indirect owner.
  • The LLC has more than one owner. It is usually a partnership and files Form 1065 instead. See Form 1120 vs Form 1065.
  • An earlier year was missed. Each missing year needs its own complete package. See late filings and catch-up.

Frequently asked questions

What does a completed Form 5472 look like for a single-member LLC?

It has three pieces: a pro forma Form 1120 showing only the name, address, EIN and item E, a Form 5472 with Parts I, II, III and VII completed and the Part V box checked, and a statement describing the contributions, distributions and formation costs for the year.

Where do I report money I put into my LLC on Form 5472?

For a foreign-owned single-member LLC, the instructions place contributions, distributions and formation costs in Part V. You check the Part V box and attach a statement that describes each transaction and its amount.

Do I need an ITIN to file Form 5472?

No. If the owner has no SSN or ITIN, line 4b(1) is left blank and a reference ID number is entered on line 4b(2). The owner's foreign tax number goes on line 4b(3), or "None" if they do not have one.

What is a reference ID number on Form 5472?

It is an identifier the LLC creates for its foreign owner, made of letters and numbers only, up to 50 characters. No IRS approval is needed, but the same reference ID must be used on every year's Form 5472.

Which parts of Form 1120 does a foreign-owned LLC fill in?

Only the name and address of the LLC and items B and E on the first page, with "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top. The income, deduction and tax lines are left blank, and the return is signed.

Can I copy this sample for my own return?

You can use it to understand the layout, but every entry must come from your own records. The names, numbers and amounts here are fictional, and a return that is substantially incomplete or inaccurate can be treated as not filed.
