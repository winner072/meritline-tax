Form 5472

Can I File Form 5472 Myself? A Practical Guide for Foreign LLC Owners

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorOctober 29, 20268 min read

Yes, you can file Form 5472 yourself. No licence is needed to file your own company's return, and many owners of simple, single-member LLCs do it successfully. The real question is not whether you are allowed to, but whether you can get every detail right, because a return the IRS treats as incomplete carries the same $25,000 penalty as one that was never filed. This guide helps you decide honestly, and shows what doing it yourself involves.

Key points

  • It is legal and common for a foreign owner to prepare and file their own LLC's Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120.
  • DIY suits simple situations: one owner, a few transactions, every year filed on time, and no IRS letters.
  • Most DIY problems come from details: a missed transaction, the wrong address, or a section left blank.
  • There is a middle path: prepare it yourself and have a professional check it before you send it.

What doing it yourself involves

  1. Confirm the LLC has an EINThe return is filed under the company's EIN. Without one, nothing can be filed.
  2. Gather the year's recordsBank and payment account statements, receipts for anything you paid personally, and the formation documents.
  3. List every reportable transactionMoney you put in, money you took out, formation costs you paid, loans, and any payments between you and the company, with totals for the year.
  4. Complete the pro forma Form 1120Only the identifying details: name, address, EIN, and the basic boxes. "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" is written across the top.
  5. Complete Form 5472The reporting company, your details as the foreign owner, the related party, and the transactions, with a short statement describing contributions and distributions.
  6. Sign and send it correctlyFax it to the IRS at 855-887-7737 or mail it to the dedicated unit in Ogden, Utah. It cannot be e-filed. Keep the confirmation.
  7. Track the dateApril 15 for calendar-year LLCs, or October 15 if you filed Form 7004 in time, plus your state's own filing.

If every answer sits in the left column, a careful owner can usually file without help. A single answer on the right is a good reason to at least have the return checked.

The mistakes that cost DIY filers the most

  • Leaving out the formation costs. Fees you paid personally to set up the LLC are reportable, which makes the first year the one most often filed wrongly.
  • Assuming no income means no return. The filing depends on transactions with you, not on profit.
  • Sending it to the wrong place. Foreign-owned LLCs use a dedicated fax number and Ogden address, not the ordinary Form 1120 address, and cannot e-file.
  • Leaving required boxes blank. For example, your foreign tax number: if you have none, the instructions say to write "None" or "N/A". A substantially incomplete form can be treated as not filed.
  • Figures that do not match the bank statements. Every amount should be traceable to your records.
  • Missing the extension rule. Form 7004 only works if it is sent by April 15. After that date, there is no extension.
  • Forgetting the state. A perfect federal return does not cover your state annual report or franchise tax.

Why most owners choose a professional

Doing it yourself is possible, but for most owners it is not the best use of their time or the safest way to protect their business. Here is why so many hand it over:

  1. The numbers are lopsidedA professional filing starts at around $200 a year. A single return the IRS treats as incomplete can cost $25,000. Paying a small, fixed fee to remove that risk is one of the easiest decisions in running a U.S. company from abroad.
  2. The first year is the hardestFormation costs, a short first tax year, and a new EIN make year one the easiest to get wrong, and the year mistakes are most often made.
  3. Your time is worth more elsewhereLearning the instructions, rebuilding the year's transactions, and preparing the forms can take a whole day. Sending your statements to a professional takes about half an hour.
  4. A second pair of trained eyesYour return is prepared, checked against your bank statements, and shown to you before it is filed, so nothing goes to the IRS that you have not seen.
  5. Nothing falls through the cracksFederal deadlines, the Form 7004 extension, and your state annual report are tracked for you, every year, with reminders.
  6. Someone in your corner if the IRS writesIf a letter ever arrives, you already have a professional who knows your file and can respond within the deadline.
  7. Peace of mindYou focus on growing your business, knowing your U.S. obligations are handled properly and on time.

Frequently asked questions

Can I file Form 5472 myself without a CPA?

Yes. There is no requirement to use a CPA or any paid preparer to file your own LLC's Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120. What matters is that the return is complete, accurate, and sent to the correct IRS address on time.

Can I file Form 5472 with tax software?

Not in the usual way. A foreign-owned single-member LLC cannot e-file Form 5472. The return is completed on the IRS forms and sent by fax to 855-887-7737 or by mail to the dedicated Ogden, Utah address.

What happens if I make a mistake on Form 5472?

A substantially incomplete Form 5472 can be treated as not filed, which can bring the $25,000 penalty. Smaller errors can usually be fixed by filing a corrected return. Acting quickly is the safest approach.

Who signs the pro forma Form 1120?

The return is signed by you as the owner of the LLC, under penalties of perjury. If a paid preparer prepares it, they also sign in the preparer section.

Can you check a Form 5472 I prepared myself?

Yes. Send us your completed return and supporting statements, and we will review it and tell you exactly what, if anything, needs changing before you file. Ask for a quote for a DIY review.
