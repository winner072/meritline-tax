Form 5472 Extension: How to File Form 7004 for a Foreign-Owned LLC

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorOctober 25, 20266 min read

A foreign-owned U.S. LLC can move its Form 5472 deadline from April 15 to October 15 by filing Form 7004, but only if Form 7004 reaches the IRS by April 15. The extension is automatic, costs nothing, and gives six extra months to file. It does not rescue a deadline that has already passed, and it does not extend your state filings. This guide explains exactly how it works for a foreign-owned LLC, which is handled differently from an ordinary U.S. company.

The essentials

  • Form: IRS Form 7004, using form code 12 (Form 1120), because the LLC files a pro forma Form 1120.
  • Cut-off: the original due date. For the 2026 tax year, that is Thursday, April 15, 2027.
  • New deadline: Friday, October 15, 2027 for calendar-year LLCs.
  • How to send it: fax to 855-887-7737 or mail to the IRS in Ogden, Utah. Foreign-owned LLCs must not use the regular Form 7004 address.

Why foreign-owned LLCs follow different extension rules

Most U.S. businesses e-file Form 7004 through tax software and send it to the address for their state. A foreign-owned single-member LLC is different. Under the IRS Instructions for Form 5472, it must fax or mail Form 7004 to the same dedicated IRS unit that receives its Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120. The instructions say plainly not to use the regular Form 7004 filing address.

This is the single most common extension mistake we see. An owner follows a generic guide, sends Form 7004 to Kansas City or e-files it under the wrong return type, and assumes the deadline has moved. If the extension is not valid, a return filed in the summer is simply late, and the $25,000 penalty for a late Form 5472 can apply.

For a fiscal-year LLC, the original due date is the 15th day of the fourth month after the year ends, and the extension adds six months. See Form 5472 tax years and due dates for worked examples, and our Form 5472 deadline guide for 2026 and 2027 for the full calendar.

Completing Form 7004, line by line

Form 7004 is a single page. For a foreign-owned disregarded LLC, most of it is straightforward:

  1. Name, EIN and addressUse the LLC's legal name and EIN exactly as they appear on the IRS EIN letter. The LLC cannot request an extension without an EIN, so apply early if you do not have one yet.
  2. Line 1: form code 12This tells the IRS the extension is for Form 1120, the pro forma return your Form 5472 is attached to.
  3. Lines 2 to 4These cover foreign corporations without a U.S. office, consolidated groups, and certain entities with automatic extensions. They do not usually apply to a single-member LLC.
  4. Line 5a: the tax yearEnter calendar year 2026, or the start and end dates of a fiscal year. For an LLC formed during 2026, the first year runs from the formation date to December 31, and line 5b lets you mark it as an initial return.
  5. Lines 6 to 8: tax amountsA foreign-owned disregarded LLC normally owes no tax with this return, so these lines are usually zero.

The extension is automatic. You will not normally receive an approval letter, so the fax confirmation page or postal receipt is your proof. Keep it with your tax records for at least as long as the return itself.

What Form 7004 does not extend

An extension covers one federal filing. Several other obligations keep their own dates:

  • State annual reports and franchise taxes. For example, Delaware's LLC annual tax is due June 1, and Wyoming's annual report is due on the first day of the month the LLC was formed. See our state filings service.
  • Your personal U.S. return, if you have one. If you have U.S. income that requires Form 1040-NR, that return is extended separately with Form 4868.
  • The FBAR. If the LLC or you must file an FBAR, it has its own automatic extension to October 15, with no form needed.
  • Your home-country tax return. Local deadlines are unaffected by anything you file with the IRS.

Should you extend at all?

An extension is the right choice when records are incomplete, when bank statements arrive late, or when the LLC was formed late in the year and the owner is still organising paperwork. It is free and carries no penalty.

It is not always the best choice. If your records are ready in March, filing the full return by April 15 removes the risk of forgetting a deadline six months later. We see more missed October deadlines than missed April ones, because an extension turns one date into two. If you do extend, put October 15 in your calendar the day you send Form 7004. If that date has already been missed, read how to deal with a Form 5472 late filing penalty.

Frequently asked questions

Can I file Form 7004 for my foreign-owned LLC online?

Not through the usual route. The IRS Instructions for Form 5472 tell foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entities to fax Form 7004 to 855-887-7737 or mail it to the dedicated Ogden, Utah address, and not to use the regular Form 7004 address.

What happens if I file Form 7004 after April 15?

It is not a valid extension. Form 7004 must be filed by the original due date of the return. After that date, the only option is to file the return itself as soon as possible, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one.

Does Form 7004 extend the deadline for my state annual report?

No. Form 7004 extends one federal return. State annual reports and franchise taxes, such as Delaware's June 1 LLC tax, keep their own deadlines.

Do I need to file a new Form 7004 every year?

Yes. Each extension covers one tax year only. If you need more time for the 2027 tax year, a new Form 7004 is due by April 15, 2028.
