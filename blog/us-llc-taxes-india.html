U.S. LLC Taxes for Indian Founders: Form 5472, the India–U.S. Tax Treaty, and Indian Reporting

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 25, 20267 min read

An Indian resident who is the only owner of a U.S. LLC almost always has to file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, even when the LLC made no profit. The India–U.S. tax treaty can protect your business profits from U.S. tax, but it does not remove the filing. And on the Indian side, owning a U.S. company brings its own reporting under Indian tax and foreign exchange rules. This guide sets out both sides clearly, so nothing falls between them.

Key points

  • U.S. filing: Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, due April 15 (October 15 with Form 7004), by fax or mail.
  • Penalty: $25,000 per form, per year, for a late or missing filing.
  • Treaty: in force since 1990. Business profits are generally taxable in the U.S. only with a U.S. permanent establishment.
  • India: the U.S. LLC is a foreign asset to report in your Indian return, and the money you invest in it falls under FEMA overseas investment rules.

Why Indian founders form U.S. LLCs

Indian SaaS founders, agencies, developers, and D2C brands form U.S. LLCs to sell to American customers in dollars, use Stripe and U.S. marketplaces, and look credible to U.S. clients and investors. Delaware and Wyoming are the usual choices. Many founders set up the LLC through an online formation service and only learn about the yearly U.S. filing, and the Indian reporting, months later.

The yearly U.S. filing

A single-member LLC owned by a non-U.S. person is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. Since 2017, it files Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120 every year it has transactions with its owner, such as formation costs paid by you, capital you send in, and money you take out. It is an information return that calculates no tax, filed under the LLC's EIN by fax or mail. Read Form 5472 explained, or check your situation in two minutes with our free Form 5472 checker.

The India–U.S. tax treaty: what it does for you

  1. Business profitsUnder the treaty, the business profits of an Indian resident are taxable in the U.S. only if they are attributable to a permanent establishment there: broadly, a fixed place of business in the U.S. or an agent there who habitually concludes contracts for you. A founder running the LLC from India, with no U.S. office or staff, usually has none.
  2. Reduced withholdingThe treaty lowers the standard 30% U.S. withholding on many payments. Dividends are generally limited to 15% or 25%, and interest, royalties, and fees for included services to 10% or 15%, depending on the type of payment.
  3. Claiming itBenefits must be claimed properly, usually on Form W-8BEN with the treaty article and rate stated. Some positions also need to be disclosed to the IRS.

The treaty does not cancel Form 5472. The treaty decides which country taxes income. Form 5472 is an information return, and it is due every year the LLC has reportable transactions, even if no U.S. tax is owed.

Facts that can change the U.S. tax answer: a U.S. warehouse or Amazon FBA inventory, U.S. employees or contractors acting for the business, or a U.S. office. Review these before you file.

The Indian side: four things to get right

This is where Indian founders most often need help, because Indian rules do not simply follow the U.S. view of a disregarded LLC.

TopicWhat it means for you
Foreign asset reportingA resident and ordinarily resident individual reports foreign assets, including an interest in a foreign company and its bank accounts, in Schedule FA of the Indian income tax return. Schedule FA uses the calendar year.
Overseas investment (FEMA)Money sent to set up or fund the LLC is an overseas investment under FEMA rules and is made through your authorised dealer bank, within the Liberalised Remittance Scheme limit of USD 250,000 per financial year, with the required reporting.
How India sees the LLCIndia may treat the U.S. LLC as a separate foreign company rather than "disregarded". That affects how its profits and payments to you are taxed in India, and raises questions such as where the company is effectively managed.
Indian tax yearIndia's financial year runs from April to March, and most individual returns are due by July 31. Credit for any U.S. tax paid is claimed in India under the treaty.

We prepare U.S. filings only. Work with a Chartered Accountant in India on the Indian return, FEMA reporting, and the structure, and keep your filed U.S. returns as supporting records.

Tax forms U.S. platforms will ask for

Stripe, marketplaces, and U.S. clients will normally need Form W-8BEN in your own name, showing India as your country of residence, because the LLC is disregarded and you are its foreign owner. Do not submit a Form W-9, which is for U.S. persons.

State filings

Delaware charges LLCs an annual tax due June 1. Wyoming requires an annual report by the first day of the month the company was formed. Florida's annual report is due between January 1 and May 1. New Mexico does not require an annual report from LLCs. See our state filings service.

If you have missed earlier years

Each missed year is a separate late Form 5472. Filing every outstanding year before the IRS contacts you, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one, generally gives the strongest position. Read how Form 5472 penalty abatement works or see our catch-up service.

What to keep ready each year

  • The LLC's Certificate of Formation or Articles, and the EIN letter
  • Your passport, Indian address, and PAN
  • All statements for the LLC's bank, Stripe, and marketplace accounts
  • Bank advice for every remittance you sent to the LLC under LRS
  • A dated list of money you put in and took out

Frequently asked questions

Is there a tax treaty between India and the U.S.?

Yes. The India–U.S. income tax treaty has been in force since 1990. It generally limits U.S. tax on an Indian resident's business profits to cases with a U.S. permanent establishment and reduces U.S. withholding on dividends, interest, royalties, and fees for included services.

Does the treaty mean I do not file Form 5472?

No. The treaty concerns which country taxes income. Form 5472 is an information return that a foreign-owned U.S. LLC files every year it has reportable transactions, whether or not tax is owed.

Do I have to report my U.S. LLC in my Indian tax return?

Generally, yes. A resident and ordinarily resident individual reports foreign assets, such as an interest in a foreign company and related accounts, in Schedule FA. Confirm the details with your Chartered Accountant.

Do I need RBI approval to fund my U.S. LLC?

Usually not separately, if the investment is made through your authorised dealer bank under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme and FEMA overseas investment rules, with the required reporting. Your bank and CA will confirm the documents needed.
