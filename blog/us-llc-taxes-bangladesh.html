U.S. LLC Taxes for Bangladeshi Founders: Form 5472 and the U.S.–Bangladesh Tax Treaty

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 25, 20267 min read

If you live in Bangladesh and own a U.S. LLC on your own, the IRS almost certainly expects a Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 from your company every year, even if it made no money. The United States and Bangladesh do have an income tax treaty, and it can matter for how much U.S. tax you owe. But the treaty does not cancel the yearly filing, and confusing the two is one of the most expensive mistakes Bangladeshi founders make.

Key points

  • Yearly filing: Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, due April 15 (or October 15 with Form 7004).
  • Penalty: $25,000 per form, per year, for a late or missing filing, regardless of profit.
  • Treaty: the U.S.–Bangladesh income tax treaty, signed in 2004, can limit U.S. tax on business profits and reduce withholding. It does not remove the filing.
  • Home side: Bangladesh taxes its residents, and money moved abroad falls under Bangladesh Bank foreign exchange rules.

Why so many Bangladeshi founders have a U.S. LLC

Bangladesh has one of the world's largest communities of online freelancers and software developers, concentrated in Dhaka, Chattogram, and Sylhet. A U.S. LLC lets them invoice American clients in dollars, open U.S. bank and payment accounts, and sell on marketplaces that are hard to access with a local business alone. Wyoming, Delaware, and New Mexico are the most common states. For most owners, the LLC is the first foreign company they have ever held, and the yearly U.S. reporting is unfamiliar.

The yearly federal filing

A U.S. LLC with one owner who is not a U.S. person is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. Since 2017, it must report its dealings with that owner on Form 5472, attached to a pro forma Form 1120. The return reports transactions and calculates no tax. It is filed under the LLC's EIN and sent to the IRS by fax or mail; it cannot be e-filed. The U.S.–Bangladesh tax treaty, in plain language

The treaty was signed in Dhaka in 2004 and has been in force since 2006. It decides which country may tax certain income of a person who is resident in one country and earns income from the other. Three parts matter most to LLC owners:

  1. Business profitsA Bangladeshi resident's business profits are generally taxable in the U.S. only if they are attributable to a permanent establishment there, such as a U.S. office, a fixed place of business, or certain dependent agents. A freelancer working from Dhaka through a U.S. LLC usually has no U.S. permanent establishment.
  2. Reduced withholdingThe treaty caps U.S. withholding on many payments that would otherwise face the standard 30% rate: generally 10% on interest and royalties, and 10% or 15% on dividends depending on ownership.
  3. Claiming itTreaty benefits are not automatic. They are claimed on the right form, usually Form W-8BEN with the treaty article stated, and in some cases disclosed to the IRS.

The treaty and Form 5472 are separate. The treaty is about tax. Form 5472 is an information return. Even an owner who owes no U.S. tax at all under the treaty still files Form 5472 every year their LLC has reportable transactions.

Facts can change the answer. Keeping inventory in a U.S. warehouse, hiring people in the U.S., or having someone in the U.S. sign contracts for you can create U.S. tax exposure. If any of these apply, get advice before you file.

Tax forms U.S. platforms will ask for

Payment platforms, marketplaces, and U.S. clients usually ask for a tax form before paying. Because the LLC is disregarded and you are its foreign owner, the correct form is generally Form W-8BEN in your own name, showing Bangladesh as your country of residence, not the Form W-9 that U.S. owners use. Submitting a W-9 by mistake can lead to your income being reported to the IRS as if you were a U.S. taxpayer.

State filings

Your LLC also answers to its state. Wyoming requires an annual report by the first day of the month the company was formed. Delaware charges LLCs an annual tax due June 1. Florida's annual report is due between January 1 and May 1. New Mexico does not require an annual report from LLCs. A missed state filing can lead to late fees and, eventually, dissolution. The Bangladeshi side

Bangladesh taxes its residents on their income, through the National Board of Revenue. The Bangladeshi income year runs from July to June, and individual returns are generally due by the end of November. Moving money out of Bangladesh, and holding investments abroad, is governed by Bangladesh Bank foreign exchange rules, which can require approval in some cases. Some income from IT and freelance services has also received special tax treatment in recent years. We prepare U.S. filings only, so confirm the Bangladeshi position with a local tax adviser, and keep your filed U.S. returns as supporting records.

If you have missed earlier years

Many Bangladeshi owners hear about Form 5472 a year or two after forming the company. Each missed year is a separate late return. The strongest position is to file every outstanding year before the IRS writes to you, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one. Read how Form 5472 penalty abatement works or see our catch-up service.

What to keep ready each year

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN letter
  • Your passport details, address in Bangladesh, and TIN if you have one
  • All statements for the LLC's bank and payment accounts
  • A dated list of money you put in and took out
  • Receipts for company costs you paid personally

Frequently asked questions

Is there a tax treaty between the U.S. and Bangladesh?

Yes. The U.S.–Bangladesh income tax treaty was signed in 2004 and has been in force since 2006. It can limit U.S. tax on a Bangladeshi resident's business profits and reduce U.S. withholding on interest, dividends, and royalties, but it does not remove the Form 5472 filing.

Does the treaty mean I do not have to file Form 5472?

No. The treaty decides which country may tax certain income. Form 5472 is an information return, and a foreign-owned U.S. LLC with reportable transactions still files it every year.

All my clients are outside the U.S. Do I still file?

Most likely, yes. Form 5472 depends on transactions between you and your LLC, such as funding it or paying yourself, not on where your clients are.

Do I need an SSN or ITIN to file from Bangladesh?

No. Form 5472 and the pro forma Form 1120 are filed under the LLC's EIN. An ITIN is needed only if you personally must file a U.S. income tax return.
