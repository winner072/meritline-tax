U.S. LLC Taxes for Ghanaian Founders: Form 5472 and Yearly Filings

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 21, 20267 min read

Key points

  • If you live in Ghana and are the only owner of a U.S. LLC, the company generally must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 with the IRS every year, including years with no revenue.
  • For calendar-year companies the filing is due on April 15. Filing Form 7004 by that date moves it to October 15.
  • A missed or incomplete Form 5472 can cost $25,000 per form, per year.
  • Ghana and the United States have no income tax treaty, so U.S. domestic rules decide withholding and whether any U.S. tax is due.
  • For Form 5472, your Ghana Card PIN is your Ghanaian tax identification number, because it replaced the old TIN for individuals in 2021.

From Accra to Kumasi and Takoradi, a growing number of Ghanaian entrepreneurs use a U.S. LLC to sell software, digital products, and services to international customers, to be paid in dollars, and to open accounts with U.S. payment and marketplace platforms. The formation process is quick and often handled entirely online. The annual obligations that come with the company are less well known, and the most important of them is a federal information return that many owners first hear about from a penalty notice. This guide sets out what a Ghanaian owner must file, the points that are specific to Ghana, and how to keep the company in good standing.

Your annual IRS obligation

A U.S. LLC with one owner who is not a U.S. person is classified as a foreign-owned disregarded entity. It does not file a normal income tax return, but it is treated as a corporation for one purpose: reporting its dealings with its foreign owner. That report is Form 5472, and it is submitted attached to a pro forma Form 1120 on which only the company's basic details are completed.

The return reports transactions, not profit, and there is usually no tax to pay with it. It is filed under the LLC's EIN and cannot be submitted through standard e-filing. Our guide Form 5472 Explained covers the form in detail, and Form 5472 tax years and due dates shows how to work out your deadline, including in your first year.

Transactions Ghanaian owners commonly overlook

Form 5472 captures money and value moving between you and your company in either direction. For owners based in Ghana, the following are the transactions most often missed:

  • Costs paid before the LLC had a bank account. Formation fees, registered agent fees, and the first months of software or hosting are frequently paid from a personal foreign currency account or a dollar card. These are contributions from you to the company.
  • Top-ups through mobile money or fintech apps. Converting cedis and sending dollars to the LLC, whether through a bank, a mobile money service, or an international transfer app, is a contribution even when the amount is small.
  • Withdrawals to Ghana. Moving profit from the LLC's account to your personal account in Ghana is a distribution to you and must be reported.
  • Company costs on a personal card, and the reverse. Each instance is a transaction between you and the LLC.

Record the dollar amount at the time of each transfer. With the cedi exchange rate moving frequently, reconstructing values months later is difficult. A simple dated log of every transfer, with the amount in U.S. dollars and its purpose, saves considerable time at filing.

U.S. tax without a Ghana–U.S. treaty

Because there is no income tax treaty between Ghana and the United States, three practical rules follow for a Ghanaian owner:

  • U.S. tax depends on whether you have a U.S. trade or business. There is no treaty "permanent establishment" threshold to rely on. If you work from Ghana and the LLC has no U.S. office, staff, dependent agents, or inventory, there is often no U.S. income tax to pay. U.S.-based people or stock held in a U.S. warehouse can change this.
  • Standard 30% withholding applies to certain U.S.-source payments, such as dividends and royalties, without any treaty reduction.
  • No treaty benefits to claim on Form W-8BEN.

None of this changes the Form 5472 requirement, which applies whether or not any U.S. tax is due.

Which tax form to give U.S. platforms

Before paying you, U.S. clients and platforms will normally ask for a tax certification. As the foreign owner of a disregarded LLC, you generally provide Form W-8BEN as an individual, in your own name. A Form W-9 is for U.S. persons, and giving one in error can lead to your income being reported to the IRS as if you were a U.S. taxpayer.

Practical points when filing from Ghana

  • EIN without an SSN. The IRS online EIN tool is not available to most non-U.S. owners. The EIN is usually obtained with Form SS-4, sent by fax or mail, or by telephone through the IRS line for international applicants.
  • Your Ghanaian tax number. Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign taxpayer identification number. For individuals in Ghana this is the Ghana Card PIN.
  • Sending the return. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS. Owners in Ghana usually use an online fax service and keep the delivery confirmation as proof of the filing date.
  • Time zone. Ghana runs on GMT, four to five hours ahead of U.S. Eastern time depending on the season. Deadlines are measured in U.S. time, but filing a few days early removes any doubt.
  • No travel needed. You do not need a U.S. visa, SSN, or ITIN to file Form 5472 for your LLC.

State requirements

Separate from the IRS, your LLC must meet the requirements of the state where it was formed:

StateWhat is due each year
WyomingAnnual report and license tax, due on the first day of the anniversary month of formation.
DelawareAnnual LLC tax, due June 1.
FloridaAnnual report, filed between January 1 and May 1.
New MexicoNo annual report for LLCs; the registered agent must be kept in place.

Unpaid state fees or reports can lead to penalties and eventually to the company losing its good standing. See our state filings and annual reports page.

Other U.S. reporting to be aware of

  • FBAR. If your LLC holds a bank or financial account outside the United States, including one in Ghana in the company's name, and the combined balance exceeded $10,000 at any time in the year, the LLC must file an FBAR (FinCEN Form 114).
  • Beneficial ownership (BOI). At the time of writing, U.S.-formed companies are exempt from FinCEN's beneficial ownership reporting. Form 5472 remains a separate IRS obligation.

Your position in Ghana

Under Ghana's Income Tax Act, 2015 (Act 896), individuals who are resident in Ghana are generally taxed on their worldwide income, which can include profits earned through a U.S. LLC. An individual is generally resident if present in Ghana for 183 days or more in a 12-month period, among other tests. How the LLC's profits should be reported to the Ghana Revenue Authority depends on your circumstances, so confirm the Ghanaian treatment with a local tax adviser. Meritline Tax handles the U.S. filings; the records you keep for Form 5472 will support your Ghanaian return as well.

Catching up on missed years

If your LLC has been running for a year or more without a Form 5472 filing, each missed year is a separate return that is now late. Filing all outstanding years together, before the IRS makes contact, and including a reasonable cause statement where the facts justify it, generally puts you in the strongest position. Read what to do after a missed Form 5472 deadline, or see our catch-up service.

Documents to prepare each year

  • Articles of Organization and the IRS letter confirming your EIN
  • Your passport details, Ghanaian address, and Ghana Card PIN
  • Statements for each LLC bank, payment processor, and marketplace account
  • A dated log of contributions to, and withdrawals from, the company
  • Receipts for company expenses you paid personally, including through mobile money or a dollar card

Frequently asked questions from Ghanaian owners

What do I enter as my foreign tax ID on Form 5472?

For individuals resident in Ghana, the Ghana Card PIN serves as the taxpayer identification number, having replaced the separate TIN in 2021.

Does Ghana have a tax treaty with the United States?

No. There is no income tax treaty in force between Ghana and the United States, so standard U.S. rules apply, including 30% withholding on certain U.S.-source payments and no treaty claim on Form W-8BEN.

Do I need to file if my LLC earned nothing?

Usually, yes. The requirement is triggered by transactions between you and the company, such as paying its formation fees or funding its account, not by profit.

Can I pay company costs through mobile money?

You can, but each payment you make for the company from personal funds is a reportable contribution. Keep the transaction records and note the dollar value at the time.

Do I have to travel to the United States to file?

No. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS, and all documents can be shared with us online.
