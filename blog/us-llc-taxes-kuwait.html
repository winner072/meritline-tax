U.S. LLC Taxes for Founders in Kuwait: Form 5472, No Tax Treaty, and Rules for Expatriates

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 25, 20266 min read

If you live in Kuwait and are the only owner of a U.S. LLC, the IRS expects a Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 from your company every year, even though Kuwait itself charges no personal income tax. There is no income tax treaty between the United States and Kuwait. And because most people living in Kuwait are expatriates, the country you hold a passport from may have its own rules about your U.S. company. This guide brings the three pieces together: the U.S. filing, the no-treaty position, and your home country.

Key points

  • Yearly U.S. filing: Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, due April 15 (October 15 with Form 7004), by fax or mail.
  • Penalty: $25,000 per form, per year, for a late or missing filing.
  • No U.S.–Kuwait income tax treaty: no treaty-reduced withholding and no treaty permanent establishment protection.
  • Expatriates: your home country may still expect you to report or pay tax on the LLC.

Who this guide is for

This guide is for founders living in Kuwait City, Hawalli, Salmiya, and across the country, whether Kuwaiti nationals or expatriates, who are not U.S. citizens or green card holders and are the sole owner of a U.S. LLC. Typical owners are consultants, engineers with side businesses, e-commerce sellers, and digital agencies using a Wyoming or Delaware LLC to bill international clients and use U.S. payment platforms.

The yearly federal filing

A single-member LLC with a foreign owner is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. Since 2017, it files Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120 each year it has transactions with its owner: formation fees you paid, money you sent in, and money you took out. The return calculates no tax and is filed under the LLC's EIN by fax or mail, not e-file. Read Form 5472 explained, or use our free Form 5472 checker.

What having no tax treaty means

The United States and Kuwait have no income tax treaty, so only U.S. domestic law applies to you:

  1. Business incomeYou are taxed in the U.S. only on income effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business. Services you perform from Kuwait, with no U.S. office, staff, or inventory, usually produce no U.S. tax.
  2. U.S. investment incomeU.S. dividends, royalties, and certain other payments face the standard 30% withholding, with no treaty rate to reduce it.
  3. Form W-8BENYou still give U.S. platforms a Form W-8BEN in your own name showing Kuwait as your residence, but leave the treaty section blank.

The risk of U.S. tax rises with Amazon FBA inventory in U.S. warehouses, people in the U.S. working for the business, or a U.S. office. Review those facts carefully, because there is no treaty to fall back on.

The Kuwaiti side

Kuwait does not levy personal income tax. Corporate income tax applies mainly to foreign corporate bodies doing business in Kuwait, while Kuwaiti and GCC-owned companies face other levies. Running business activity inside Kuwait can also involve commercial licensing and residency rules. We prepare U.S. filings only, so confirm the Kuwaiti position with a local adviser.

State filings

Wyoming requires an annual report by the first day of the month the company was formed. Delaware charges LLCs an annual tax due June 1. Florida's annual report is due between January 1 and May 1. New Mexico does not require an annual report from LLCs. See our state filings service.

If you have missed earlier years

Each missed year is a separate late Form 5472. Filing all outstanding years before the IRS contacts you, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one, is generally the strongest position. Read how Form 5472 penalty abatement works or see our catch-up service.

What to keep ready each year

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN letter
  • Your passport, Kuwait Civil ID, and address
  • All statements for the LLC's bank and payment accounts
  • A dated list of money you put in and took out
  • Details of any U.S. inventory, staff, or office

Frequently asked questions

Is there a tax treaty between the U.S. and Kuwait?

No. The United States and Kuwait do not have an income tax treaty, so U.S. tax and withholding for Kuwait residents follow U.S. domestic law without treaty reductions.

Kuwait has no income tax. Do I still file Form 5472?

Yes, in most cases. Form 5472 is a U.S. information return based on transactions between you and your LLC, and it applies regardless of whether Kuwait taxes you.

I am an expatriate in Kuwait. Does my home country tax my U.S. LLC?

It depends on your home country's residence rules. Some countries stop taxing foreign income once you live abroad long enough, while others continue to require reporting. Check the guide for your country and confirm with a local adviser.

Will I pay U.S. tax on my LLC's income?

Often not, if all work is done from Kuwait and the business has no U.S. office, staff, or inventory. Income effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business is taxable, judged under U.S. law alone.
