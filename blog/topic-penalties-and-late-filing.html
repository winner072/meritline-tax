Form 5472 Penalties and Late Filing: How to Catch Up and Request Relief

Updated October 26, 20262 guides

The penalty for a late, missing, or substantially incomplete Form 5472 is $25,000 per form, per year, and it can grow by a further $25,000 for each 30 days a failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notice. It is one of the largest fixed penalties a small company can face. It can also be reduced or removed where there is reasonable cause, and what you do first makes a real difference.

What to do, by situation

Your situationFirst step
Never filed, no IRS contactFile every outstanding year together, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support one.
Just missed October 15File now; there is no further extension. Read what to do after a missed deadline.
Penalty notice receivedRespond within the time limit on the notice with a written abatement request. Read how abatement works.
Request refusedAsk about review by the IRS Independent Office of Appeals, within the time limit.

Why filing before the IRS contacts you matters

The continuation penalty only starts after IRS notification, and an owner who comes forward and files every missing year with a clear, honest explanation is generally in a stronger position than one who waits. Relief is never guaranteed, and simply not knowing about the form is rarely enough on its own, but well-documented facts give the IRS a basis to grant it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the penalty for not filing Form 5472?

$25,000 per form, per tax year, plus $25,000 for each 30-day period the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notice, with no maximum. Relief may be available for reasonable cause.

Should I wait to see if the IRS notices a missed Form 5472?

No. Filing before the IRS contacts you is generally the strongest position, and the continuation penalty only begins after IRS notification.
