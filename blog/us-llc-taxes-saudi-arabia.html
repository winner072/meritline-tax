U.S. LLC Taxes for Founders in Saudi Arabia: Form 5472 and Yearly Filings

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 21, 20267 min read

Key points

  • If you live in Saudi Arabia and are the sole owner of a U.S. LLC, the company is generally required to file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, whatever your nationality.
  • The deadline for calendar-year LLCs is April 15, or October 15 if Form 7004 is filed on time.
  • The IRS penalty for a missing or substantially incomplete Form 5472 is $25,000 per form, per year.
  • Saudi Arabia has no income tax treaty with the United States, so U.S. domestic rules apply in full.
  • The form records your country of citizenship and your country of residence separately, which matters for the many expatriates who run U.S. LLCs from the Kingdom.

Saudi Arabia's founder community is unusually international. Saudi nationals building digital ventures under Vision 2030 sit alongside Indian, Pakistani, Egyptian, Filipino, and other expatriate professionals who run consulting practices, agencies, and online stores from Riyadh, Jeddah, and Dammam. Many choose a U.S. LLC to contract with overseas clients, receive payments in dollars, and use U.S. payment and marketplace platforms. Whatever your passport, if you are the company's only owner and you are not a U.S. person, the same annual IRS obligation applies. This guide explains that obligation and the points that are specific to owners based in Saudi Arabia.

The annual U.S. filing

A single-member LLC whose owner is not a U.S. person is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. It is disregarded for U.S. income tax, but it is treated as a corporation for information reporting. Each year it must file Form 5472, reporting its transactions with you, attached to a pro forma Form 1120 on which only basic company information is completed.

The return discloses transactions; it does not compute tax. It is filed under the LLC's EIN and is faxed or mailed to the IRS, since standard e-filing is not available for this return. For background, see Form 5472 Explained and Form 5472 tax years and due dates.

Citizenship and residence: a point expatriates should get right

Form 5472 asks for the owner's country of citizenship and, separately, the country in which the owner files income tax returns as a resident. An Indian national living in Riyadh, for example, would generally show India as the country of citizenship and Saudi Arabia as the country of residence, unless they file as a tax resident elsewhere. Completing these lines accurately avoids inconsistencies with the information you give banks and platforms.

Your home country may still have a view. If you are an expatriate in Saudi Arabia, the country of your nationality may have its own residence rules, and in some cases may tax income earned through a foreign company. Confirm your position there as well, particularly if you spend significant time in your home country.

Transactions to record during the year

Every transfer of money or value between you and the LLC is a reportable transaction. Owners in Saudi Arabia most often create them by:

  • paying formation, registered agent, and early operating costs from a personal Saudi bank card or account;
  • sending funds from a personal account to the LLC's U.S. bank or payment account;
  • withdrawing profit to a personal account in the Kingdom or in your home country;
  • using a personal card for company expenses, or the company card for personal ones.

Because the Saudi riyal is pegged to the U.S. dollar, converting amounts is straightforward, but you should still keep a dated record of each transfer in dollars.

U.S. tax without a treaty

As there is no Saudi–U.S. income tax treaty, the U.S. applies its ordinary rules. U.S. income tax generally arises only on income connected with a U.S. trade or business and on certain U.S.-source payments. An owner who works from Saudi Arabia, with no U.S. office, employees, dependent agents, or inventory, often owes no U.S. income tax, although the facts must be assessed case by case. Certain U.S.-source payments, such as dividends and royalties, face the standard 30% withholding rate, and there is no treaty benefit to claim on Form W-8BEN. Form 5472 is required either way.

The form to give U.S. platforms

When a U.S. client, payment processor, or marketplace asks for a tax form, the owner of a foreign-owned disregarded LLC generally provides Form W-8BEN as an individual, in their own name. Form W-9 is only for U.S. persons. Giving a W-9 by mistake can result in your income being reported to the IRS as if you were a U.S. taxpayer.

Practical points for owners in Saudi Arabia

  • EIN. Owners without a U.S. Social Security Number usually obtain the LLC's EIN using Form SS-4, sent by fax or mail, or by telephone through the IRS line for international applicants.
  • Foreign tax number. Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign taxpayer identification number, if one exists. Many individuals in Saudi Arabia do not hold a personal tax number, so discuss with your preparer how the line should be completed.
  • Working week and time zone. Saudi Arabia is seven to eight hours ahead of U.S. Eastern time, and its weekend falls on Friday and Saturday. Plan your filing so it is sent well before the U.S. deadline.
  • No travel required. No U.S. visa, SSN, or ITIN is needed to file Form 5472 for your LLC.

Other U.S. reporting

  • FBAR. A U.S. LLC is treated as a U.S. person for FBAR. If it has accounts outside the United States, such as a Saudi bank account in the company's name, totalling more than $10,000 at any point in the year, FinCEN Form 114 is due.
  • BOI. At the time of writing, companies formed in the United States are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting. This does not affect Form 5472.

The Saudi side

Saudi Arabia does not levy a general personal income tax on individuals' employment income. Business activity can, however, fall within the Kingdom's corporate income tax or zakat rules, administered by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA), depending on how and where it is carried on and on the nationality of the owners. Whether income from a U.S. LLC run from Saudi Arabia has any Saudi tax or zakat consequence is a question for a Saudi adviser. Meritline Tax prepares the U.S. filings.

Missed earlier years

If your LLC has operated for one or more years without filing Form 5472, each year is a separate late return. Filing every outstanding year promptly, before IRS contact, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support it, is generally the best course. Read what to do after a missed Form 5472 deadline, or see our late filings service.

Documents to prepare each year

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN confirmation letter
  • Your passport, your address in Saudi Arabia, and your country of citizenship
  • Any tax identification number you hold in your country of residence
  • Statements for every LLC bank, payment, and marketplace account
  • A dated list of funds you put into, and took out of, the company

Questions owners in Saudi Arabia ask

I am an expatriate in Saudi Arabia. Which country do I show on Form 5472?

The form separates citizenship from tax residence. You generally show your nationality as the country of citizenship and the country where you file as a tax resident, often Saudi Arabia, as the country of residence.

Is there a U.S.–Saudi Arabia tax treaty?

No. There is no income tax treaty in force, so standard U.S. rules apply, including 30% withholding on certain U.S.-source payments and no treaty claim on Form W-8BEN.

My LLC had no revenue. Must it still file?

Usually, yes. The requirement depends on transactions between you and the company, such as paying its formation costs or funding its account, rather than on profit.

I do not have a personal tax number in Saudi Arabia. Is that a problem?

Not necessarily. Form 5472 asks for a foreign tax number if one exists. Your preparer will complete the line in line with the IRS instructions.

Can everything be done from Saudi Arabia?

Yes. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS, and your documents can be shared with us online.
