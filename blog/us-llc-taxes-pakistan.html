U.S. LLC Taxes for Pakistani Founders: What to File Each Year

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 21, 20267 min read

Key points

  • A U.S. LLC owned solely by a resident of Pakistan generally has to file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, even in years with no profit.
  • The U.S. return follows the LLC's U.S. tax year, normally the calendar year, and is due April 15 (October 15 with Form 7004). Pakistan's July–June tax year does not change this.
  • The penalty for a late or substantially incomplete Form 5472 is $25,000 per form, per year.
  • Pakistan and the United States have an income tax treaty. It can affect U.S. tax and withholding, but it does not remove the Form 5472 filing.

Pakistan has one of the largest freelance workforces in the world, and its software houses, digital agencies, and Amazon and Shopify sellers increasingly operate through U.S. LLCs. A U.S. company makes it easier to sign international contracts, receive payments in dollars, and work with U.S. payment and marketplace platforms. What often goes unexplained is the annual information return that the IRS expects from every foreign-owned single-member LLC. This guide covers that obligation, how the Pakistan–U.S. tax treaty fits in, and the practical issues that owners in Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, and elsewhere in Pakistan should know.

The IRS filing for foreign-owned LLCs

When a U.S. LLC has one owner who is not a U.S. person, it is classified as a foreign-owned disregarded entity. For income tax it is disregarded, but for reporting it is treated as a corporation, and it must disclose its transactions with you on Form 5472, attached to a pro forma Form 1120.

This is an information return. It is filed under the LLC's EIN, cannot be submitted through standard e-filing, and is faxed or mailed to the IRS. For a detailed walkthrough, read Form 5472 Explained.

Two different tax years

Pakistan's tax year runs from July 1 to June 30. Your U.S. LLC, however, generally uses the tax year its owner uses for U.S. tax purposes, which for most Pakistani owners with no U.S. filing requirement is the calendar year. Your Form 5472 therefore covers January to December, even though your Pakistani return covers July to June. Keep records that can be split both ways. Our guide to Form 5472 tax years and due dates explains the U.S. rule in full.

Reportable transactions for Pakistani owners

Any transfer of money or value between you and the LLC is reportable. Common examples for owners in Pakistan include:

  • Start-up costs paid personally, such as formation and registered agent fees paid with a personal card or through a payment service before the LLC had its own account.
  • Funding the LLC from your personal account or freelance earnings to cover tools, advertising, or inventory.
  • Remitting profit to Pakistan, whether through a bank, a payment provider, or a remittance service. Each transfer from the LLC to you is a distribution.
  • Mixing payouts. If marketplace or client payments for the LLC's work arrive in a personal account, or the reverse, the resulting transfers must be accounted for.

Keep the money trail clear. Receiving the LLC's income into the LLC's own account, and moving profit to Pakistan in documented transfers, simplifies Form 5472 and supports the documentation Pakistani banks and the tax authorities may expect for foreign remittances.

What the Pakistan–U.S. tax treaty does

The income tax treaty between Pakistan and the United States allocates taxing rights between the two countries. For an owner of a U.S. LLC, its main effects are:

  • Business profits. Under the treaty, business profits of a Pakistani resident are generally taxable in the U.S. only if they are attributable to a permanent establishment in the United States. Working from Pakistan without a U.S. office or dependent agent usually means no permanent establishment exists.
  • Withholding. The treaty may reduce U.S. withholding on certain U.S.-source income, such as dividends or royalties. Any reduction must be claimed, usually in Part II of Form W-8BEN.
  • Disclosure. If you are engaged in a U.S. trade or business but rely on the treaty to avoid U.S. tax, you may need to file a U.S. non-resident return, Form 1040-NR, with Form 8833 disclosing the treaty position.

The treaty does not cancel Form 5472. It deals with tax, while Form 5472 is an information requirement that applies regardless.

Forms U.S. platforms will request

U.S. clients, payment processors, and marketplaces typically request a tax form before paying. As the Pakistani owner of a disregarded LLC, you generally complete Form W-8BEN as an individual, in your own name, and you may claim treaty benefits in Part II where they apply. Form W-9 is for U.S. persons only; supplying one in error can cause your income to be reported to the IRS as U.S. income.

Practical points when filing from Pakistan

  • EIN without an SSN. Most Pakistani owners obtain the LLC's EIN using Form SS-4, by fax or mail, or by telephone through the IRS line for international applicants.
  • Your Pakistani tax number. Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign taxpayer identification number. For individuals in Pakistan, the CNIC number is typically used as the tax registration number with the Federal Board of Revenue.
  • Time difference. Pakistan is nine to ten hours ahead of U.S. Eastern time. Aim to fax your return well before the U.S. deadline.
  • No U.S. visit, SSN, or ITIN is required to file Form 5472 for your LLC.

Other reporting

  • FBAR. A U.S. LLC is a U.S. person for FBAR purposes. If it holds accounts outside the United States, such as a Pakistani account in the company's name, totalling more than $10,000 at any point during the year, FinCEN Form 114 must be filed.
  • BOI. At the time of writing, U.S.-formed companies are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting. This does not affect Form 5472.

Your position in Pakistan

Individuals who are resident in Pakistan are generally taxed on their worldwide income, subject to relief under tax treaties. You are generally resident if you are present in Pakistan for 183 days or more in a tax year. Pakistan has also offered special tax treatment for IT and IT-enabled service exports where the proceeds are received through formal banking channels, which may be relevant if your LLC earns income from software or digital services. How LLC profits should be reported to the Federal Board of Revenue depends on your circumstances, so confirm the treatment with a Pakistani tax adviser. Meritline Tax prepares the U.S. filings.

Catching up on missed years

If Form 5472 has not been filed for earlier years, each year is a separate late return. Filing all outstanding years before the IRS makes contact, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support it, is generally the strongest approach. See what to do after a missed Form 5472 deadline and our catch-up service.

Records to keep ready

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN letter
  • Your passport, Pakistani address, and CNIC number
  • Statements for every LLC bank, payment, and marketplace account, organized by calendar year
  • A dated record of money moved between you and the company
  • Receipts for company costs you paid personally

Questions from Pakistani owners

Does the Pakistan–U.S. tax treaty mean I do not have to file Form 5472?

No. The treaty affects how income is taxed, but Form 5472 is an information return. A foreign-owned U.S. LLC with reportable transactions still files each year.

My Pakistani tax year is July to June. Which period does Form 5472 cover?

The LLC's U.S. tax year, which for most Pakistani owners is the calendar year, January to December.

What tax number do I enter on Form 5472?

Your Pakistani taxpayer identification number. For individuals, the CNIC number is typically used as the tax registration number with the Federal Board of Revenue.

My LLC made no money. Must it still file?

Usually, yes. Paying formation costs or funding the company's account creates reportable transactions, regardless of profit.

Can I file without travelling to the U.S.?

Yes. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS, and documents can be shared with us online.
