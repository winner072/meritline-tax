U.S. LLC Taxes for Egyptian Founders: Filings and the Tax Treaty

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 21, 20266 min read

Key points

  • Egyptian residents who are the sole owners of a U.S. LLC generally need the company to file Form 5472 and a pro forma Form 1120 every year.
  • The deadline for calendar-year LLCs is April 15, which Form 7004 extends to October 15.
  • A late or substantially incomplete Form 5472 can attract a $25,000 penalty per form, per year, whether or not the LLC made money.
  • Egypt and the United States have an income tax treaty. It can limit U.S. tax and withholding, but it does not cancel the Form 5472 filing.

Egyptian developers, designers, content creators, and online merchants are increasingly selling to customers in the Gulf, Europe, and North America, and a U.S. LLC is a popular way to do it. The company makes it easier to invoice in dollars, receive international payments, and open accounts with U.S. payment and marketplace platforms. It also brings an annual IRS information return that many owners only learn about later. This guide sets out what owners in Cairo, Alexandria, Giza, and elsewhere in Egypt must file, how the Egypt–U.S. tax treaty applies, and the practical considerations specific to Egypt.

The IRS return for foreign-owned LLCs

A U.S. LLC that has one owner who is not a U.S. person is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. Although it is disregarded for U.S. income tax, it is treated as a corporation for information reporting. Each year it must report its dealings with its owner on Form 5472, attached to a pro forma Form 1120.

The return is an information filing made under the LLC's EIN. It cannot be e-filed and is sent to the IRS by fax or mail. See Form 5472 Explained and Form 5472 tax years and due dates for more detail.

Transactions Egyptian owners need to report

Form 5472 captures every transfer of money or value between you and your company. Owners in Egypt most often create reportable transactions by:

  • paying formation, registered agent, and initial subscription costs with a personal card, often in foreign currency;
  • transferring personal funds to the LLC's U.S. account to cover operating costs;
  • moving profit from the LLC to a personal account in Egypt;
  • paying the LLC's suppliers or advertising costs personally.

Record dollar values when money moves. The Egyptian pound has seen significant movements against the dollar, and Form 5472 is completed in U.S. dollars. Keeping a dated record of each transfer in dollars avoids conversion problems when the return is prepared.

How the Egypt–U.S. tax treaty works for LLC owners

The tax treaty between Egypt and the United States allocates taxing rights between the two countries. For an Egyptian-resident owner of a U.S. LLC, it generally means:

  • Business profits are taxable in the U.S. only to the extent they are attributable to a permanent establishment there. If you run the business from Egypt, without a U.S. office or a dependent agent, there is usually no permanent establishment.
  • Withholding on certain U.S.-source income, such as dividends and royalties, may be reduced from the standard 30% where the benefit is claimed in Part II of Form W-8BEN.
  • Treaty disclosure. If you are engaged in a U.S. trade or business but rely on the treaty to avoid U.S. tax, you may need to file Form 1040-NR with Form 8833.

The treaty addresses tax. It does not remove Form 5472, which applies to every foreign-owned LLC with reportable transactions.

Tax forms for U.S. clients and platforms

U.S. clients, payment processors, and marketplaces will usually request a tax form. As the Egyptian owner of a disregarded LLC, you generally provide Form W-8BEN in your own name as an individual, and you may claim treaty benefits in Part II where applicable. Form W-9 is reserved for U.S. persons.

Practical points for owners in Egypt

  • EIN. Owners without an SSN usually obtain the LLC's EIN using Form SS-4, sent by fax or mail, or by telephone through the IRS international line.
  • Your Egyptian tax number. Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign tax identification number. If you are registered with the Egyptian Tax Authority, use your tax registration number.
  • Time difference. Egypt is roughly six to seven hours ahead of U.S. Eastern time, depending on daylight saving in both countries. File well ahead of the deadline.
  • No travel needed. No U.S. visa, SSN, or ITIN is required to file Form 5472 for your LLC.

Other U.S. reporting

  • FBAR. A U.S. LLC is a U.S. person for FBAR purposes. If the company holds accounts outside the United States, including an Egyptian account in its name, with balances totalling more than $10,000 at any point in the year, FinCEN Form 114 is required.
  • BOI. At the time of writing, companies formed in the United States are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting.

Your position in Egypt

You are generally treated as resident in Egypt if Egypt is your habitual place of abode, or if you are present there for more than 183 days in a 12-month period. Whether, and how, profits from a U.S. LLC should be reported in Egypt depends on your circumstances and on Egyptian law, with the treaty available to relieve double taxation where income is taxed in both countries. Confirm the Egyptian treatment with a local adviser or the Egyptian Tax Authority. Meritline Tax prepares the U.S. filings.

Bringing missed years up to date

Each year for which Form 5472 was required but not filed is a separate late return. Filing all outstanding years together, before the IRS makes contact, and including a reasonable cause statement where justified, generally gives you the best footing. Read what to do after a missed Form 5472 deadline or see our late filings service.

Documents to keep ready

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN letter
  • Your passport, address in Egypt, and Egyptian tax registration number if you have one
  • Statements for each LLC bank, payment, and marketplace account
  • A dated record, in U.S. dollars, of contributions and withdrawals
  • Receipts for company expenses you paid personally

Questions Egyptian owners ask

Does the Egypt–U.S. tax treaty mean I do not have to file Form 5472?

No. The treaty deals with which country may tax certain income. Form 5472 is an information return, and a foreign-owned U.S. LLC with reportable transactions must still file it each year.

Can the treaty reduce my U.S. tax?

It can. Business profits are generally taxable in the U.S. only if attributable to a permanent establishment there, and withholding on certain payments may be reduced when claimed on Form W-8BEN.

How do I handle Egyptian pound amounts?

Form 5472 is completed in U.S. dollars. Record the dollar value of each transfer at the time it happens.

Does an LLC with no income still file?

Usually, yes. Paying the company's costs or funding its account yourself generally creates reportable transactions.

Can I complete the process from Egypt?

Yes. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS, and documents can be shared with us online.
