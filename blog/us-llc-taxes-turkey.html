U.S. LLC Taxes for Turkish Founders: Form 5472 and the Tax Treaty

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorUpdated October 21, 20266 min read

Key points

  • A U.S. LLC wholly owned by a resident of Türkiye generally files Form 5472 together with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, profitable or not.
  • For calendar-year LLCs the return is due April 15; Form 7004 extends the deadline to October 15.
  • Not filing, or filing a substantially incomplete form, can result in a $25,000 penalty per form, per year.
  • Türkiye and the United States have an income tax treaty, which can reduce U.S. tax and withholding but does not remove Form 5472.
  • With the lira's frequent movements, recording the U.S. dollar value of every transfer at the time is especially important.

Türkiye's technology, design, and e-commerce sectors have embraced international markets, and a U.S. LLC has become a common structure for Turkish founders who sell software, digital services, and physical products abroad. It helps with dollar invoicing, access to U.S. payment processors and marketplaces, and credibility with overseas clients. Alongside these benefits comes an annual U.S. information return that is often overlooked until a penalty notice arrives. This guide explains what owners in Istanbul, Ankara, İzmir, and elsewhere in Türkiye need to file, and how the tax treaty affects them.

The federal return every foreign-owned LLC files

A U.S. LLC with a single non-U.S. owner is a foreign-owned disregarded entity. It is ignored for U.S. income tax, but treated as a corporation for reporting, and it must disclose its transactions with you on Form 5472, attached to a pro forma Form 1120.

The return is filed under the LLC's EIN and is faxed or mailed to the IRS; electronic filing is not available for it. Read Form 5472 Explained for detail and Form 5472 tax years and due dates to confirm your deadlines.

Reportable transactions for owners in Türkiye

All movements of money or value between you and the LLC are reportable. The most common for Turkish owners are:

  • Setup costs paid personally, such as formation and registered agent fees charged to a personal card.
  • Funding the LLC from a personal foreign currency account or by converting lira to dollars.
  • Distributions when you move profit from the LLC to your personal account in Türkiye.
  • Inventory and supplier payments made personally for an LLC's e-commerce business, which are contributions to the company.

Use dollar values, recorded at the time. Form 5472 is completed in U.S. dollars. Given how quickly the lira can move, reconstructing values later from lira statements risks errors. Keep a dated record of each transfer in dollars.

The Türkiye–U.S. tax treaty in practice

The income tax treaty between Türkiye and the United States sets out which country may tax particular types of income. For a Turkish-resident owner of a U.S. LLC:

  • Business profits are generally taxable in the U.S. only if they are attributable to a permanent establishment there. Operating from Türkiye, without a U.S. office or dependent agent, typically means no permanent establishment.
  • Withholding on certain U.S.-source income, such as dividends and royalties, may be reduced below the standard 30% when the benefit is claimed in Part II of Form W-8BEN.
  • Disclosure. Where you are engaged in a U.S. trade or business but rely on the treaty to avoid U.S. tax, a Form 1040-NR with Form 8833 may be needed.

None of these benefits removes the Form 5472 filing, which is a separate information requirement.

What to give U.S. platforms

When asked for a tax form, the Turkish owner of a disregarded LLC generally provides Form W-8BEN in their own name as an individual, claiming treaty benefits in Part II where they apply. Form W-9 is only for U.S. persons; giving one in error can cause income to be reported to the IRS as U.S. income.

Practical points when filing from Türkiye

  • EIN. Without an SSN, owners usually obtain the LLC's EIN using Form SS-4, by fax or mail, or by telephone through the IRS international line.
  • Your Turkish tax number. Form 5472 asks for the owner's foreign tax identification number. For Turkish citizens, the national identity number (T.C. Kimlik No.) is used for tax purposes; foreign residents use the tax number issued by the Revenue Administration.
  • Time difference. Türkiye is seven to eight hours ahead of U.S. Eastern time, depending on U.S. daylight saving. File several days ahead of the deadline.
  • No travel required. You do not need a U.S. visa, SSN, or ITIN to file Form 5472 for your LLC.

Other U.S. reporting

  • FBAR. A U.S. LLC is a U.S. person for FBAR. If it holds accounts outside the U.S., such as a Turkish account in the company's name, totalling more than $10,000 at any time in the year, FinCEN Form 114 is due.
  • BOI. At the time of writing, U.S.-formed companies are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting.

Your position in Türkiye

Individuals resident in Türkiye are taxed on their worldwide income. You are generally treated as resident if you are domiciled in Türkiye or stay there for more than six months in a calendar year. Profits from a U.S. LLC can therefore be relevant to your Turkish return, with treaty relief available to prevent double taxation where income is taxed in both countries. Confirm how your LLC income should be reported with a Turkish tax adviser or the Revenue Administration (Gelir İdaresi Başkanlığı). Meritline Tax prepares the U.S. filings.

If years have been missed

Each unfiled year is a separate late Form 5472. Filing all outstanding years before IRS contact, with a reasonable cause statement where the facts support it, is generally the most effective path. See what to do after a missed Form 5472 deadline and our catch-up service.

Records to prepare

  • The LLC's Articles of Organization and EIN letter
  • Your passport, address in Türkiye, and Turkish tax or identity number
  • Statements for every LLC bank, payment, and marketplace account
  • A dated record, in U.S. dollars, of transfers between you and the LLC
  • Receipts for company costs paid personally, including inventory

Questions from owners in Türkiye

Does the Türkiye–U.S. tax treaty remove the need for Form 5472?

No. The treaty affects taxation, while Form 5472 is an information return. A foreign-owned U.S. LLC with reportable transactions still files every year.

Which Turkish number goes on Form 5472?

For Turkish citizens, the T.C. Kimlik No. is used for tax purposes. Foreign residents of Türkiye use the tax number issued by the Revenue Administration.

How should I convert lira amounts?

Form 5472 is completed in U.S. dollars. The simplest approach is to record the dollar value of each transfer when it happens, rather than converting lira figures later.

Does an LLC with no profit still file?

Usually, yes. Paying the company's formation costs or funding its account is generally enough to require a filing.

Can the filing be done from Türkiye?

Yes. The return is faxed or mailed to the IRS, and all documents can be shared with us online.
