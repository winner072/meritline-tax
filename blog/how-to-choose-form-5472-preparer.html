Hiring a Form 5472 Preparer: 8 Questions to Ask First

By Mmesoma Winner, Managing DirectorNovember 27, 20265 min read

Before you pay anyone to file Form 5472, ask who prepares and reviews it, what credentials they hold, what the fee covers, how it is filed, and what happens if the IRS writes to you. A Form 5472 filing is not a routine tax return, and a missed or incomplete one can carry a $25,000 penalty. These eight questions help you compare preparers and spot problems before you pay.

Key points

  • Ask who actually prepares your return. A paid preparer must have an IRS PTIN and sign the return.
  • Know what their credentials allow. Only CPAs, enrolled agents, and attorneys have unlimited rights to represent you before the IRS.
  • Get a fixed fee in writing. Ask what it covers and what is charged separately.
  • Ask how you will get proof of filing. You should receive confirmation for every return.
  • A missed or incomplete filing costs more. The penalty is $25,000 per form, per year.

Why the preparer you choose matters

Form 5472 is an information return that a foreign-owned U.S. LLC files with a pro forma Form 1120. It asks for details of every reportable transaction between you and the company, so a preparer who does not know the form can leave out items or file the wrong return. Many firms advertise low prices, so the questions below help you compare what you actually receive.

Eight questions to ask before you hire

  1. Who prepares my return, and who reviews it?A good answer names a person, not only a company. You should know who is in charge before the return is filed.
  2. What are their credentials?A paid preparer must have an IRS PTIN. A CPA, enrolled agent, or attorney can also represent you if the IRS writes to you. Ask which applies and ask for the name.
  3. Do they prepare Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 regularly?Foreign-owned LLC filings are specialised.
  4. Ask whether they handle first-year and catch-up returns. What exactly does the fee cover?Ask for a fixed price in writing, and whether the pro forma Form 1120, an extension, and corrections are included.
  5. What is charged separately?State fees, registered agent fees, and state annual reports are often separate. Ask for the full list before you pay.
  6. How will the return be filed, and how will I know it was received?You should receive proof of filing for every return, and a copy of what was sent.
  7. What happens if the IRS writes to me?Ask who responds and whether that is included. Some preparers cannot represent you before the IRS.

8. How do you handle my documents? You will share bank statements and identification. Ask how files are received, stored, and deleted, and avoid sending passports by email.

How to compare two quotes

  • Two prices are only comparable if they cover the same work. Check these points on each quote:
  • Whether the pro forma Form 1120 is included
  • Whether the price covers one tax year or all the years listed
  • Whether state fees and the registered agent are shown separately
  • When payment is due, and what happens if you stop halfway Whether corrections to the return are included

Red flags to watch for

  • A price far below everyone else’s, with no explanation
  • No named preparer or reviewer
  • A promise that you will never owe a penalty
  • No questions about your transactions with the LLC
  • Pressure to decide or pay immediately

What to have ready

Having your records ready makes any preparer faster and cheaper. These are the usual items:

  • Your EIN letter and Articles of Organization
  • Business bank or Wise statements for the year
  • A list of amounts you paid personally for the LLC
  • Your contributions to the LLC and any money you took out

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a CPA to file Form 5472?

No. A paid preparer needs an IRS PTIN and must sign the return, but it does not have to be a CPA. A CPA, enrolled agent, or attorney can also represent you if the IRS writes to you, so ask who would do that.

Can I prepare Form 5472 myself?

Yes, but the form is detailed, and a missed or incomplete filing carries a $25,000 penalty. If you file it yourself, check every transaction between you and the LLC.

Is the cheapest preparer a good choice?

Not automatically. A very low price can mean no review, no proof of filing, or extra charges later. Compare what each fee covers, not only the amount.

How much does a Form 5472 filing cost?

Our filing starts from $200 per tax year for an LLC with no business activity, and from $250 with activity. The pro forma Form 1120 is included.

When should I hire a preparer?

Before the April 15, 2027 deadline for a calendar-year LLC, and ideally in January while your records are fresh. Form 7004 can extend the deadline to October 15, 2027.
