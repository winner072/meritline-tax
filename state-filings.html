Three kinds of state filing

  • Annual report. A short yearly filing with the state where the company was formed, with a state fee. It keeps the company active on the state's records.
  • Franchise tax. A yearly charge some states make simply for the company existing there, whether or not it earned anything.
  • State tax return. A return that reports income to a state. It is needed only when the business operates or earns income in that state.

These are separate from your federal filing. A company can be up to date with the IRS and still fall out of good standing with its state.

What popular states require

These are the states most owners abroad choose. The amounts below are the state's own fees, and we last checked them against the official state sources on November 9, 2026. State fees change from time to time, so we confirm the current amount when we quote.

Wyoming

Annual report · due each year

Due by the first day of the month the company was formed. The state charges an annual license tax with the report: $60 or 0.02% of the company's assets in Wyoming, whichever is more. A company with $300,000 or less of assets in Wyoming pays $60. If the report is not filed within 60 days after the due date, the state dissolves the company.

Delaware

Annual tax · due June 1

LLCs pay a flat annual tax of $400 and file no annual report. The tax for each year is due by June 1 of the next year, and the full $400 is due even if the company existed for only part of the year. Paying late adds a $200 penalty plus interest of 1.5% a month on the tax and penalty. Corporations file an annual report with franchise tax by March 1.

New Mexico

No annual report for LLCs

New Mexico does not require LLCs to file an annual report, so there is no yearly report fee. This is one reason owners abroad choose it. The company must still keep a registered agent in New Mexico, and the federal filing still applies.

Florida

Annual report · January 1 to May 1

Filed between January 1 and May 1 each year, starting the year after the company is formed. The state fee for an LLC is $138.75. The state adds a $400 late fee after May 1.

Formed somewhere else? We file in other states too, including California, New York, New Jersey, Texas, Illinois, Georgia, and Massachusetts.

Common questions

Is an annual report the same as a tax return?

No. An annual report is a short yearly filing with the state where your company was formed. It confirms details such as the company's address and registered agent and comes with a state fee. A tax return reports income. Many LLCs owe an annual report even when they owe no tax.

What happens if I miss my state filing?

States charge late fees, and after a period of non-filing they can dissolve the company or mark it as not in good standing. That can cause problems with banks and payment providers.

Who pays the state fee?

You do. State fees and any state tax owed are separate from our filing fee. On request, we make the payment to the state on your behalf.

My state is not listed. Can you still help?

Yes. We check the rules for your state and file what is due.
