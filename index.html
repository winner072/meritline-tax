Frequently asked questions

Answers for foreign owners of U.S. LLCs

Still unsure what you need to file? Ask us and we will guide you.

Does my foreign-owned U.S. LLC need to file Form 5472 if it had no income?

In most cases, yes. A foreign-owned single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 for any year it has reportable transactions with its owner. Paying formation or state fees, or funding the company's bank account, usually counts, so having no revenue does not by itself remove the requirement.

What happens if Form 5472 is not filed?

The IRS penalty for failing to file Form 5472 starts at $25,000 per form, per year. If you have missed prior years, we can help you catch up and, where it applies, prepare a reasonable cause statement.

When is Form 5472 due?

For calendar-year companies, Form 5472 and the pro forma Form 1120 are generally due April 15. Filing Form 7004 before that date extends the deadline to October 15.

Does my LLC also have to file with its state?

Often, yes. Wyoming and Florida require an annual report, and Delaware charges LLCs a yearly tax. New Mexico does not require an annual report from LLCs. We check the rules for your state and file what is due.

What documents do I need for Form 5472?

Usually your formation documents, EIN confirmation letter, owner details, and bank statements showing money the owner put in or took out during the year. After you request a quote, we send a checklist tailored to your company.

Yes. Most of our clients live outside the U.S. and own a U.S. company. We work entirely online, by email and WhatsApp, wherever you are.
