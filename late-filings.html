How far back do you need to go?

Normally to the year the LLC was formed. A foreign-owned single-member LLC has had to file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2017, and for every year it had transactions with its owner. Paying the formation costs or funding the bank account is usually enough, so the first year is the one most often missed.

There is a second reason to go all the way back. When a required information return is not filed, the IRS's usual three-year window to assess that year generally does not start. An unfiled year stays open, so filing it is the only way to close it.

What happens after you file late

Every case is different, and no one can promise a particular outcome. In practice, one of these usually follows:

  • No further contact. The returns are processed and nothing more is heard. Keep your filing confirmations safely.
  • A penalty notice arrives, sometimes weeks or months later. It states the amount and a deadline to respond. A written abatement request is prepared and sent within that period.
  • A request for more information. The IRS asks a question about a return or the statement, and a clear, documented answer is sent back.

Whatever happens, responding on time matters. If a request for relief is refused, there may be a further right of appeal. Read how Form 5472 penalty abatement works.

The reasonable cause statement

A reasonable cause statement is a written explanation filed with the late returns. It asks the IRS not to apply the penalty because the delay was not due to neglect. A strong statement is:

  • Specific: it sets out your own timeline, from formation to filing, with dates.
  • Honest: every fact matches your records, and it is signed under penalties of perjury.
  • Supported: it refers to evidence such as advice you received or the date you learned of the requirement.
  • Forward-looking: it shows what you have done to stay compliant from now on.

Not knowing about Form 5472 is common and is an honest explanation, but on its own it is rarely enough. It carries more weight alongside other facts and prompt action once you found out. We write each statement from your actual circumstances, never from a template.

Common questions

I have never filed Form 5472. Is it too late?

No. Missed years can still be filed, and there is no deadline after which a late return is refused. Acting before the IRS contacts you generally puts you in a stronger position than waiting.

How many years do I need to file?

Usually every year since the LLC was formed in which it had transactions with you, starting no earlier than tax years beginning in 2017. Paying formation costs or funding the bank account normally means the first year must be filed too.

Will the penalty be removed?

No one can promise that. Where you had a genuine reason for the delay, we prepare a reasonable cause statement asking the IRS for relief. The IRS decides each request on its facts.

Can I file only the latest year and leave the older ones?

It is not advisable. Each unfiled year remains open and carries its own penalty exposure. Filing all missing years together, with one consistent explanation, is generally the stronger approach.
